Dobra · 1860–1893 · a new story from the registers

Neighbors of the Mosaic Faith

From 1871 to 1877, the parish priest of Dobra also entered in the baptism register children he did not baptize: births in Jewish families.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

In 1871–1877 the parish priest of Dobra also entered in the baptism register children whom he did not baptize. These were births in Jewish families living in Dobra. In the religion column the priest wrote “Judaica,” he left the baptism-date column empty, and in the margin he added: “Israelitica confessionis,” of the Mosaic faith.

The register contains two such families. Szymon and Sprinca Fischler, of house no. 139 and later no. 225, had a son here, Izaak (1871), and daughters Laja (1874) and Rifka (1877). Józef Menk, son of Majer and Ernestyna, née Stein, was the propinator in Dobra, that is, the leaseholder of the right to sell liquor. He and his wife Regina had three daughters in house no. 202: Anna (1873), Blüme (1874), and Zytel (1876). For the last two the priest added the Polish equivalents of their names in parentheses: Bolesława and Józefa.

Almost all these children were delivered by the same midwife, Rozalia Przybytek of Dobra. More about midwives is told in the story “Midwives without Schooling”.

The last such entry dates from May 1877. Soon afterward Jewish births began to be recorded in separate registers kept for the Mosaic faith.

Baptisms

The registers also record baptisms of Jews. On April 29, 1860, in house no. 99 in Dobra, 26-year-old Anna from Jordanów in the Wadowice district was baptized. Her surname can be read only tentatively, as Pirger. She took the names Katarzyna Józefa, and the godparents were Jan and Lidia Klima (surname read with uncertainty). In the same house no. 99, five years earlier, the doctor sent from Vienna had died; he is the subject of the story “The Doctor from Vienna”.

On July 26, 1893, three-year-old Zygmunt Władysław was baptized, son of Izaak Bier, the stationmaster of the railroad station in Dobra, and Sara, née Toker. That same day the boy died of diphtheria, and the parish priest buried him. The godfather was the railroad official Włodzimierz Dobrowolski. Whether the parents asked for the baptism, the register does not say.

The parish registers show only those moments when Jewish neighbors came into contact with the parish. Other sources tell of their daily life, their inns, and their shops.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe entries of Jewish births from 1871–1877, the baptism of 1860, and the baptism of 1893. The transcriptions were checked against the scans.
  • To be checkedSome surnames and first names, for example Pirger, Zytel, and the surname of Regina Menk’s father.

What we don’t know

  • How many Jewish families lived in the parish, and since when.
  • Why for several years their children were recorded in the register of a Catholic parish.
  • What became of the Fischler and Menk families.

Sources
