the whole parish · 1786–1896 · a new story from the registers

Death in Childbirth

The death registers of the parish’s nine villages show how often childbirth ended in the mother’s death, and what then became of the child and the family.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

The priest recorded the cause of death briefly. For women who died in childbirth or a few days after it, he wrote “in partu,” in childbirth, “propter partum,” because of childbirth, or “ob infelicem partum,” because of an unhappy delivery.

One in a hundred

In the nine villages of the parish, almost eighteen thousand children were baptized in 1786–1896. In the same years the registers recorded about ninety deaths of married women in childbirth or in the lying-in period, that is, within six weeks after it. In the second half of the 19th century, for which the entries are more complete, this works out to roughly one mother’s death per 120 to 130 births (as of November 2026).

For a single birth that is not much, but a woman usually gave birth many times, so the risk added up. Almost one in five married women aged 20 to 44 whose deaths the registers recorded died in childbirth or shortly after it. The mothers who died were on average 32–33 years old.

Thirteen days

On May 4, 1875, in Chyszówki, in house no. 64, Marianna Tomera, née Syrzeń, gave birth to a son, Józef. The midwife Agnieszka Gdowicz attended the birth, and the next day the boy was baptized. The mother was 39. Thirteen days later she was dead.

Four days after his mother, little Józef died. He was 17 days old, and the priest entered weakness as the cause, as he did for so many of the infants in the story “The First Year of Life”.

The widower, 32-year-old Jan Tomera, remarried on July 20, 1875, two months after his wife’s funeral; his new wife was 26-year-old Marianna Smoleń. They were related, so they needed a dispensation from the bishop in Tarnów. They received it on June 29, six weeks after Marianna’s death. How quickly widowers remarried is the subject of the story “Widows and Widowers”.

The midwife and the sacraments

Midwives helped at births, often without any schooling, as told in the story “Midwives without Schooling”. For several of the mothers who died, the priest noted that they had received the sacraments in time: “sacramentaliter provisa.”

Before 1850

In the registers from 1786–1849 there are clearly fewer such deaths, about three per thousand births. We do not know whether mothers died less often then or whether the priests less often entered the cause. The same problem arises with the oldest entries about infants.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe 1875 entries about the Tomera family: Józef’s baptism, his mother’s death, the child’s death, and the father’s remarriage. Checked against the scans.
  • ProbableThe figures, as of November 2026, from all nine villages of the parish. Adding Gruszowiec, Porąbka, Wilczyce, Zadziele, and Łostówka did not change the proportions. Mothers’ deaths were counted by machine, from the cause entered by the priest and by linking a death with a child’s baptism within six weeks. The priest recorded some such deaths without a cause, so the true numbers were probably higher.

What we don’t know

  • How many mothers died after childbirth of a fever that the priest recorded simply as fever or as natural death.
  • Who fed and raised the children who outlived their mothers.

Sources

  • Baptism, marriage, and death registers of all the villages of the parish from 1786–1896 (collections 4268 and 4269), including, for Chyszówki: baptisms, ref. 4269/3510 (scan 041); deaths, ref. 4269/5505 (scans 016, 017); marriages, ref. 4269/4500 (scan 044); on the Metryki website of the Polish Genealogical Society (PTG).
