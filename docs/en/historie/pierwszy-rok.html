four villages of the parish · 1840–1889 · a new story from the registers

The First Year of Life

More than 5,000 baptisms and 1,000 infant deaths in the registers of Dobra, Jurków, Chyszówki, and Półrzeczki show how many children died before the age of one, when the danger was greatest, and what the priest recorded as the cause.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

In 1840–1889 more than five thousand children were baptized in Dobra, Jurków, Chyszówki, and Półrzeczki. In the same years the death registers of these villages recorded more than a thousand infant deaths, that is, deaths of children who had not yet reached their first birthday. That is one in five of the children born. Nearly one in three did not live to see their fifth birthday.

Eleven days

Jakub, son of Józef Mrożek and Anna, née Bielska, was born on April 28, 1884, in Półrzeczki, in house no. 6. The next day the curate Jan Czopek baptized him. Eleven days later the boy died. The priest entered the cause in a single word.

Next to Jakub’s baptism entry the priest later added a cross and the date of his death: “+ 9/5 1884.”

The word “debilitas,” weakness, is the most common cause of infant death in these years. The priest entered it, or a similar term, for more than a third of them. Much could hide behind it: premature birth, a congenital defect, an illness no one could name. The priest was not a doctor.

The first month was the most dangerous

More than two-fifths of the infants who died did not survive four weeks. More of them died in the first week alone than in the three months from the fourth to the sixth.

What they died of

After weakness, the priest most often wrote a “natural” or “ordinary” death, without naming any disease. This is the case for one in six of the infants who died. Next come cough and whooping cough, convulsions, lung diseases, and throat diseases: tonsillitis and diphtheria, which in our area was called “sipka.” It is the subject of the story “Sipka”. Less often dysentery, smallpox, and measles. Smallpox is the subject of the story “Smallpox and Vaccination”.

Boys and girls

Boys died more often. Almost 23 of every 100 baptized boys died before their first birthday, and 18 of every 100 girls. This is so everywhere and still is today: in the first year of life, boys are weaker.

Time of year

Most infants died in February, the fewest in October and November. This is partly because the most children were born in winter. That is the subject of the story “A Calendar of Births”.

Stillborn

From the 1830s the priest also recorded stillborn children in the death register. They had no names. The entry began with the word “puer” or “puella,” boy or girl, and the cause column read “in utero matris suffocatus,” suffocated in the mother’s womb. In 1840–1889 there are 237 such entries, more than four per hundred births.

In several dozen of them the priest noted that the child had been washed with water while still in the mother’s womb: “in utero matris aqua ablutus.” This was an emergency baptism, usually given by the midwife. Midwives are the subject of the story “Midwives without Schooling”, and twins, who died even more often, of the story “Twins”.

Before 1840

In the older registers, from 1786–1839, there are clearly fewer infant deaths: about 15 per 100 born. We do not know whether fewer children really died then, or whether the priests recorded them less carefully. Before 1830 they hardly recorded stillbirths at all, and they sometimes gave the age of small children as a bare number, without saying whether it meant days, weeks, or years.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe entries about Jakub Mrożek, checked against the scans. The general picture: one child in five died before its first birthday, most often in the first month of life.
  • ProbableThe curate’s surname. In this entry the first letter is unclear and it looks like “Łopek,” but in several legible entries from 1880–1888 the same curate Jan signed himself Czopek (as of November 2026).
  • ProbableThe exact numbers and percentages. They were counted by machine from the transcriptions of the baptism and death registers and may differ slightly. For Dobra the years 1840–1879 were used, because the later death register has not yet been fully transcribed.
  • PossibleWhat lay behind the words “weakness” and “natural death.”

What we don’t know

  • Whether every infant who died made it into the death register, especially in the oldest registers.
  • How many children born in the parish died elsewhere, for example when their parents went away for work.
