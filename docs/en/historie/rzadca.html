The Dobra estate · 1734–1739 · a new story from the registers and court records

Forty-One Charges

From 1734 to 1739 the Dobra estate was run by the nobleman Kazimierz Święcicki. When he left, the lord’s commissioner drew up 41 charges against him. One concerns the spoils taken after the capture of a famous brigand chief.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

The Dobra estate was not always run by the lord of the manor himself. In 1734 the Małachowski family leased it to a Mr. Wielogłowski, who hired a nobleman, Kazimierz Święcicki, as administrator, that is, steward. Over the following years the steward made life hard for people from the whole estate: from Dobra, Jurków, Chyszówki, Gruszowiec, and Zadziele.

A register of wrongs

In 1739, when the lease ended, the lord sent his commissioner, Jan Dzierżek, to Dobra. People waited for hours outside the manor house to tell what had happened to them. From this the commissioner drew up 41 charges and took the case to the court in Nowy Sącz.

Many of the charges concerned work the steward had not paid for, even though it went beyond the “pańszczyzna,” the labor the peasants owed the manor. He did not pay the blacksmith Błażej Macka, nor the Kuchta family for carting salt to Nowy Targ, nor Pająk and Natkański for shingles. The miller Jan Brzezula ground two sacks of grain for him every week for four years and got one hog for it. The steward also took people’s belongings: a chain from Józef Ranosz of Zadziele, a goat from Wojciech Gąsior, and a cow from the cottager Andrzej Michalczak. He had Mikołaj Michałek of Chyszówki locked up over an axe.

Among the wronged was Maciej Podgórniak of Jurków, whom the steward did not pay for carting grain. In December 1738 his daughter Agnieszka was baptized in Jurków. In 1733–1764 this is the only Podgórni family in the whole parish. He is most likely my ancestor, the forefather of the house no. 5 line. The priests recorded him sometimes as Maciej, sometimes as Mateusz.

Loot from the outlaw chief

Charge number 27 read differently from the rest. There was circumstantial evidence against the steward that he had kept for himself some of the stolen goods recovered after the capture of the outlaw chief Baczyński.

Józef Baczyński from Skawica, near Babia Góra mountain, was the leader of a band of highland outlaws, a “harnaś.” His band raided manor houses, inns, and parsonages. According to studies of highland banditry, he was captured in the fall of 1735 during a revel at an inn on the Dobra estate and executed in Kraków at the turn of 1735 and 1736. The charge of 1739 links this affair with the manor in Dobra. The outlaw himself, and what Jurków remembers of his capture, are the subject of the story “The Brigand Chief’s Last Revel”.

“But I did not kill him”

The steward answered the charges in a letter of August 16, 1739. He did not deny that he had beaten people. He only explained why. Of one of the overseers he wrote: “I gave him a few lashes, but I did not kill him.” Of a night watchman he had found asleep, he wrote that he had struck him with a stick.

The gravest charge concerned the deaths of four people. They were Marcin Brudziana, a manor farmhand; the old cooper Wróbel and his son; and Wojciech Judowski. According to the suit, the steward had hastened their deaths with cruel beatings and imprisonment. Wojciech Judowski of Dobra is also known from the baptism register: his daughter Marianna was baptized on March 7, 1730.

On September 3, 1739, the starost’s court in Kraków summoned the leaseholder to bring the former steward before it. He was sued jointly by the lord of the manor, Jan Małachowski, by his commissioner, and by the heirs of the dead: of Brudziana, of Judowski, and of Michał and Wojciech Wróbel. The historian Edward Wojtusiak called it an unusual alliance of wronged peasants and a cheated lord.

The steward’s wife

In 1737–1738 the wellborn Marianna Święcicka of Dobra appears several times in the baptism register as a godmother. Once she stood godmother together with the parish priest. She was most likely the steward’s wife. In his letter the steward mentions that it was “my wife” who had the sick farmhand taken to the bakehouse to warm up.

The register also knows the stewards before and after him. In 1728–1731 children of Stanisław Budziński, administrator of the estate, were baptized. In 1742–1745 the administrator was Stanisław Szymański, and his wife Konstancja stood godmother to peasant children from Jurków.

A few years earlier the peasants of the estate had won a dispute with the parish priest, as the story “The Heaped Bushel” tells.

How certain is this

  • CertainEntries from the baptism register: the Podgorniak family in Jurków in 1738, Marianna Święcicka in 1737–1738, the Judowski family in 1730, and the administrators Budziński and Szymański. Readings compared with the scans.
  • ProbableThe charges, the steward’s reply, and the suit. We know them from the article by Edward Wojtusiak, who described the court records. Also that Marianna Święcicka was the steward’s wife, and that Maciej Podgórniak was my ancestor.
  • PossibleThe place where the outlaw chief was captured. Accounts speak of an inn in Dobra or of a hamlet called “Wilczyska,” perhaps Wilczyce.

What we don’t know

  • How the steward’s trial ended. Wojtusiak writes of the peasants’ double victory, over the parish priest and over the steward, but does not cite the verdict in this case.
  • Which Marcin Brudziana the suit meant. A man of that name from Gruszowiec was still a witness at a wedding in February 1740.
  • What was found on the outlaw chief, and what of it went missing.
