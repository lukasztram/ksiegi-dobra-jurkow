Dobra and Jurków · 16th century – 1973 · a new story from the registers and historical studies

The Icon from Jurków

A small oval painting of the Transfiguration hangs in the high altar of the old church in Dobra. Its story links Vlach shepherds, a flood, and a parish priest who built it an altar of its own.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

In the high altar of the wooden Church of Saints Simon and Jude Thaddeus in Dobra hangs a small painting of the Transfiguration of the Lord. It is painted on a wooden panel, is oval in shape, and measures 45 by 65 centimeters. In the middle is the face of Christ. For centuries it was considered miraculous. The parish venerates it especially on August 6, the Feast of the Transfiguration.

The shepherds’ church

In the 15th century, Vlach and Ruthenian shepherds came into the forests and pastures around Dobra. In time they merged with the local population, but for a long time they kept to the Eastern rite. According to tradition, they built themselves a small cerkiew, an Eastern-rite church, on the slopes of Mogielica. No one knows what it looked like. All that is known is that it held an image of the Transfigured Christ with “stern features” and “black hair,” as described by Father Edward Wojtusiak, parish priest of Dobra and a historian of its past.

Brought by the water

According to tradition, the cerkiew was destroyed by a flood. The water carried the icon down from Jurków and left it on the “kamieniec,” that is, the stony riverbank, below the church in Dobra. The parishioners went to fetch it in procession and solemnly carried it into the church.

When this happened is not known for certain. In one work Father Wojtusiak gave the years 1683–1690, and in another the year 1712. He also mentioned a flood in 1673. Adrian Cieślik, who collected these accounts, points out that the icon must have reached Dobra after 1678. That year the old church burned down with all its furnishings, as told in the story “The Register Begins with a Fire”. According to one account, after the fire the parishioners prayed for some time in the cerkiew itself. The new church was consecrated in 1684.

An altar from the parish priest

In 1692 Father Wojciech Juraszewski became parish priest of Dobra. He held the post for forty years and furnished the new church: he brought in an organ, gilded and painted the altars, and for the icon of the Transfiguration set up a separate, fourth altar in the middle of the church. Father Wojtusiak called him a promoter of regional devotions. The same parish priest carried on a dispute with his parishioners over the meszne, the dues in grain owed to the parish priest, which is the subject of the story “The Heaped Bushel”. The godmother’s name was Cerkowa. This name appears in the registers in Jurków and Dobra from 1682, and in the 18th century also in Gruszowiec. It may come from “cerkiew,” but just as well from something else entirely. The registers do not say.

The copy and the return

In 1865 the painter Jan Stankiewicz of Oświęcim, who was then restoring the church’s paintings, painted a copy of the icon. The copy went into the altar, and the original to the rectory.

In 1973 the painting was conserved under the supervision of the diocesan curia in Tarnów and the monuments conservator in Kraków. The work was led by Professor Zofia Medwecka. After the conservation the copy was taken down, and the 16th-century original returned to the altar. On the Feast of the Transfiguration, August 6, 1973, the painting visited Jurków.

The story “Water” asked whether the icon from Jurków survived. It did, and it hangs in the high altar of the old church in Dobra.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe 1694 entry, checked against the scan. The painting in the high altar, the 1865 copy, and the 1973 conservation, according to Adrian Cieślik’s study and the description of the church.
  • ProbableThat the icon came from the cerkiew of the Vlach and Ruthenian shepherds and that a flood brought it to Dobra. This is a tradition written down by Father Edward Wojtusiak.
  • To be checkedThe year of the flood: 1673, 1683–1690, or 1712, depending on which of Father Wojtusiak’s works one follows.
  • PossibleA connection between the name Cerkowa and the word “cerkiew.”

What we don’t know

  • Where exactly the cerkiew stood and what it looked like.
  • Who painted the icon, and when.
