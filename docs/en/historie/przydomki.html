the whole parish · 1690–1911 · a new story from the registers

Aliases

“Alias,” “vel,” “recte”: with these words the priest added a second surname. Sometimes it was an office, sometimes a nickname, and sometimes the name of the homestead.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

Sometimes one surname was not enough. The priest added a second one: “alias,” otherwise; “vel,” or; “recte,” properly. The parish registers contain several dozen such pairs.

Wojciech, known as Wójt

The oldest pair dates from 1690. That year the godfather of a child from Półrzeczki was Wojciech Przybytek “alias Woyt,” from Dobra. Wojciech Przybytek the wójt (village head) is known from court records: soon afterward he burned the parsonage’s measure for the meszne, the grain tribute, as the story “The Heaped Bushel” tells. The office stuck to him like a second surname.

Two surnames for one family

In 1735 a child was held at the font by Wojciech Galas “alias Fleszar” from Dobra. On the same page there is a Małgorzata Fleszar, so the byname already lived on as a surname in its own right. In Chyszówki, from 1808 on, the priest several times recorded the Chudziak family “alias Marczak.”

Two sisters, Tekla and Anna, daughters of Wojciech and Regina, née Dudzik, also lived in Chyszówki. When Tekla died in 1888, the priest recorded her as “Węgrzyn alias Gąsior.” When Anna died in 1911, a different priest wrote “Węgrzyn recte Gąsior”: properly Gąsior. In twenty-three years the village had not settled which surname was the real one.

Other pairs

  • Dobra · 1789Jan Maston alias Mysza.
  • Półrzeczki · 1828Wojciech Rapacz vel Firas.
  • Dobra · 1833Józef Bolisęga alias Jarosz, an uhlan (cavalry lancer), who appears in the story “The Emperor’s Soldiers”.

Where did second surnames come from? Sometimes from an office, as with Wójt. Sometimes from an occupation or a nickname. Often from the house: in many villages of the region people were named after the farmstead, and a man who married into it in time took on the name of the house. In the same way the surnames of old families passed to parts of villages, as the story “Hamlets Named for Families” tells.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe pairs from 1690, 1735, 1808, 1833, 1888, and 1911. Entries compared with the scans.
  • ProbableThat the Wojciech Przybytek “alias Woyt” of 1690 is the village head who appears in the records of the dispute with the parish priest.
  • PossibleThe explanations of where particular bynames came from.

What we don’t know

  • What the byname Fleszar meant and where it came from.
  • Which surname the people recorded under two names used in everyday life.
