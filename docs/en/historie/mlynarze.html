the whole parish · 1683–1917 · a new story from the registers

The Millers

From Tomasz Młynarz in the 17th century to Jan Radziszewski in 1917, the registers recorded the parish’s millers, their wives, and their children.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

The villages of the parish lie in the valleys of streams flowing down from the mountains. Their water drove mills, as the story “Water” mentions. The registers do not describe the mills, but they do record the millers: when they held children at baptism, witnessed weddings, or died. In Latin a miller is “molitor”; sometimes the priest wrote “molarius,” and in the 20th century, in Polish, “młynarz.”

Tomasz Młynarz

In the oldest registers, from the 1680s, Tomasz Młynarz of Dobra appears many times. He witnessed several weddings, in 1684 he himself married Zofia Fornal of Łostówka, and in 1687 he had his son Wojciech baptized. Whether Młynarz was already his surname or only his occupation, the registers do not say. In Jurków at that time there lived a Kazimierz recorded in Latin as “Molitor,” that is, Młynarz, and also the Młynarczyk family, whose name means the miller’s children. We still find this surname in the parish in the 20th century.

The miller and the steward

In 1739 the peasants of the Dobra estate drew up complaints against the steward. For four years the miller Jan Brzezula of Dobra had ground two sacks of grain a week for him and got only a young pig in return. This is told in the story “Forty-One Charges”. In the same years the manor in Dobra was in a dispute with the monks over the boundary with Stróża, and the miller from Stróża, Jan Piechówka, testified in the case.

The Stokłosa family

The registers record the most millers at the end of the 18th century, and several of them bore the surname Stokłosa. In 1788 the miller Kazimierz Stokłosa held a child at baptism in Dobra, in 1791 the miller Walenty Stokłosa did so in Gruszowiec, and in 1796 the miller Józef Stokłosa in Jurków.

Millers’ wives also stood as godparents. In 1789–1792 Gertruda Stokłosa, “uxor molitoris,” the miller’s wife, was godmother to five children in Dobra. In 1791 in Jurków the godmother was Dorota Stokłosa, also a miller’s wife.

In the same years the registers record still other millers: Andrzej Gąsior, of Jurków in 1784 and of Dobra in 1795, and Sebastian Cech in Dobra in 1790. Perhaps there were several mills here, or perhaps several millers worked in one. The registers do not say.

In the 19th and 20th centuries

Later, millers appear in the registers less often. In 1835 the 78-year-old Bartłomiej Mycoń, “molarius,” died in Dobra, and in 1838 the 71-year-old Maria, wife of the miller Melchior Wątor. In 1843 a witness at a wedding in Dobra was Stanisław Matacz, recorded as “molarius Communitatis,” probably the village community’s miller. In January 1844 in Wilczyce, in house no. 21, Jan Chrzciciel (John the Baptist), son of Tomasz Kosmal, a miller, and Zofia, was baptized. In December 1896 the 60-year-old miller Antoni Czech died in Dobra.

In 1886 in Półrzeczki the 23-year-old Wojciech Majchrzak, son of the miller Piotr and Marianna, née Augustyniak, was married. For the underage bride, Kunegunda Olejarz, consent was given by the court in Mszana Dolna, and for the groom by his father. The miller could not write and signed with a cross. At the baptisms of Wojciech’s children in 1887–1893 the priest noted each time that their grandfather was a miller.

The last miller in the transcribed registers is Jan Radziszewski, a miller from Leńcze who lived in Dobra. In 1917 a daughter, Janina, was born to him; she died in 1919.

The millrace

Water was led to the mill wheel by a separate channel, the millrace. On June 26, 1888, in Dobra, one-and-a-half-year-old Agnieszka, daughter of Józef Mycoń and Franciszka, née Miśkowiec, drowned in one.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe entries from 1684, 1687, 1784, 1788, 1789, 1791, 1795, 1796, 1835, 1838, 1843, 1844, 1886, 1888, 1896, and 1917, checked against the scans.
  • ProbableThat “molarius Communitatis” means the village community’s miller. The information about the millers Jan Brzezula and Jan Piechówka, which Father Edward Wojtusiak took from 18th-century court records.
  • To be checkedThe surname of the miller of 1790: Cech or Czech.
  • PossibleThat the Stokłosas of Dobra, Jurków, and Gruszowiec were a single family of millers.

What we don’t know

  • Where the mills stood and how many there were at any one time.
  • Whether they belonged to the manor, to the village community, or to the millers themselves.
  • Whether the miller Sebastian Cech of 1790 and Antoni Czech, who died in 1896, were from the same family.
