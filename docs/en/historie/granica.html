Jurków · 1913–1925 · a new story from the registers and historical studies

A Parish Boundary through Jurków

For more than five hundred years, Jurków belonged to the parish of Dobra. When it began to break away in 1913, the parish priest fought over every funeral, and the boundary of the new parish cut the village in two.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

As early as the 14th century, Jurków belonged to the parish in Dobra. It was here that the children of Jurków were baptized, here that people married, and here, in the Dobra cemetery, that the dead were buried. All the stories on this site that concern Jurków come from the registers of Dobra parish.

The expositure

In 1913, after a visitation of the parish, the Bishop of Tarnów, Leon Wałęga, decided to separate Jurków from Dobra parish. An expositure (“ekspozytura”) was created: a pastoral outpost with its own priest, but not yet an independent parish. Chyszówki, Półrzeczki, Wilczyce, and Łostówka also belonged to it. That same year construction began in Jurków on a wooden church designed by the architect Jan Tarczałowicz. The work was led by Father Jan Nagórzański, a curate of Dobra parish who lived in Jurków.

The parish priest of Dobra, Father Hilary Kocańda, was firmly opposed. According to Adrian Cieślik, who has written about the affair, he feared that the income from funerals at the new cemetery in Jurków would no longer go into his coffers, and Father Nagórzański fell deeply out of his favor. The dispute dragged on for years.

A cemetery without the parish priest’s consent

Permission for the cemetery in Jurków was obtained on November 19, 1915, and the first funeral took place there on January 14, 1916. The dead of Chyszówki and Półrzeczki, however, were still entered in the registers kept in Dobra, and under the first death of 1916 in the Chyszówki register the priest left a long remark.

The person buried was six-month-old Stanisława Drzyzga, who had died of whooping cough. In the register the curate’s name is written “Nagorański,” with an exclamation mark, but historical accounts give the form Nagórzański. The remark itself is also the subject of the story “A New Cemetery without the Parish Priest’s Consent”. At the next funeral the priest wrote only: “eodem modo ut supra,” in the same way as above. For the dead from Półrzeczki he kept repeating “iure caduco,” without valid right. In November 1916 a different remark appears next to Father Nagórzański’s signature: “Licentia ab Ill. Epp. Consist.,” permission from the bishop’s consistory. So the bishop’s curia had given its consent to these funerals.

A parish, but not all of Jurków

On May 10, 1925, the parish of Our Lady of Perpetual Help was erected in Jurków. It includes Jurków, Chyszówki, Półrzeczki, and Wilczyce. In one of the church’s side altars hangs a copy of the icon of the Transfiguration of the Lord, whose original is in Dobra; the story “The Icon from Jurków” tells about it.

Not all of Jurków, however, ended up in the new parish. Its lower part, Jurków Dolny, with the chapel at Szyrszenie and my ancestors’ house no. 5, stayed in Dobra parish and belongs to it to this day. Once a year, on the Monday after Pentecost, Dobra parish celebrates Mass in the chapel in Jurków Dolny. The chapel itself is the subject of the story “The Chapel at Szyrszenie”.

Why here?

I don’t know. No source I have been able to find says why the parish boundary was drawn through the middle of the village. Distance does not explain it: the chapel at Szyrszenie is 1.4 kilometers from the church in Jurków and 2.2 kilometers from the old church in Dobra.

One can only guess. The lower end of the Jurkówka valley joins Dobra, and the people from there, like all of Jurków, had gone to church in Dobra for centuries. Father Kocańda was defending his parish and may have kept for it the part of the village that lay closest. The residents themselves may also have wanted it. The answer may lie in the bishop’s decree of 1913 and in the act erecting the parish in 1925, which should be in the Diocesan Archive in Tarnów, in the Dobra parish chronicle, or in Władysław Węglarz’s book about the building of the church in Jurków.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe 1916 entries about burials in Jurków without the parish priest’s consent and with the consistory’s license, compared with the scans. The erection of the parish in 1925, and Jurków Dolny belonging to Dobra parish today.
  • ProbableThe course of the dispute between the parish priest of Dobra and Father Nagórzański, according to the account by Adrian Cieślik, who relied on Władysław Węglarz’s book and on the Dobra parish archive.
  • To be checkedThe reading and meaning of the word “intabulato” in the 1916 remark.
  • PossibleAll the explanations of why Jurków Dolny stayed in Dobra parish.

What we don’t know

  • Why the parish boundary was drawn exactly this way, and who decided it.
  • Whether Jurków Dolny was part of the expositure at all in 1913, or stayed with Dobra from the start.
  • Exactly which houses in Jurków belong to Dobra parish.
