A Calendar of Weddings

More than 5,500 marriages in the parish registers show when weddings were held: in which month, on which day of the week, and at what age people stood before the altar.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

People did not marry at just any time. The transcribed parish registers contain more than five and a half thousand weddings with a full date, from 1682–1943 (as of November 2026). They are spread very unevenly over the months.

Carnival and fall

The most weddings were in February: more than a third of all marriages. Together with January, that is almost half. This was Carnival, or Shrovetide, the last season for merrymaking before Lent. The second peak falls in November, after the harvest, when there was grain in the barns and a few weeks were left before Advent.

In December, over two hundred and sixty years, the priest recorded only nine weddings; in April, twenty-seven; in March, ninety-five. Advent and Lent were times of penance, when no weddings were held.

Over time summer stopped being a dead season. In 1682–1779 roughly one wedding in six took place between May and October. After 1850 almost two weddings in five were held in those months.

Day of the week

In the oldest registers, up to 1779, more than half of the weddings are dated on a Sunday. From the end of the 18th century people most often married on a Tuesday, and in the second half of the 19th century on a Monday, Tuesday, or Wednesday. On a Friday, a day of fasting, almost never: in 1780–1943 only 38 times out of more than five thousand weddings.

How old they were

From 1780 on, the priest entered the ages of the bride and groom. In 1780–1849 the groom was usually 27 and the bride 22. After 1850 people married a little later: he at 29, she at 24. Before 1850 one bride in nine was not yet eighteen. The marriage of a fifteen-year-old orphan girl to the manor’s forester is told in the story “The Forester of Dobra”, and the wedding of a sixteen-year-old girl from Porąbka to an overseer from Tyrol in the story “The Railroad and the Steam Saw”.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe overall picture: February and November as the wedding months, almost no weddings in Advent and Lent.
  • ProbableThe exact numbers and percentages, as of November 2026. They were computed by machine from the transcriptions of the registers of all nine villages and may be slightly off. I counted the duplicate copies only for years for which the main registers are missing.

What we don’t know

  • Whether in the oldest registers the date always means the wedding day. The priest may sometimes have entered the day of the last banns or the day on which he made the entries.
  • How long a wedding celebration lasted. The registers record only the ceremony in church.

Sources

  • All the transcribed marriage registers of the parishes of Dobra and Jurków for 1682–1943 (collections 4268 and 4269, and for years without main registers, the duplicate copies from collection 3402) on the Metryki website of the Polish Genealogical Society (PTG).
