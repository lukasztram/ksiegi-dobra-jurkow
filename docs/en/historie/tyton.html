Półrzeczki, house no. 50 · 1842 · a new story from the registers

Hungarian Tobacco

In January 1842, Franciszek Kucaj’s father brought the priest sad news from Krościenko. His son had been killed at the Hungarian border because he would not give up his tobacco.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

In January 1842 an entry unlike any other appeared in the death register of Półrzeczki. It spans several columns, and the priest carefully noted how he knew of this death. He had not seen the dead man, nor had he buried him. The news was brought to him by the father.

Franciszek was 21 and came from house no. 50 in Półrzeczki. He was killed near Krościenko nad Dunajcem, less than 30 kilometers from home as the crow flies, not far from what was then the border between Galicia and Hungary. He was buried there, too.

Why tobacco

In the lands of the Austrian monarchy, Galicia included, tobacco had been a monopoly since 1784: only the state could manufacture and sell it. Hungary was brought under the monopoly only in 1850, and until then tobacco was grown and sold there freely. Tobacco brought from Hungary was therefore contraband in Galicia, and the border was watched by armed guards.

In those days people from the parish went to Hungary for work. One of them, who died on the Tisza in 1853, is the subject of the story “Rakamaz, 1853”. Whether Franciszek was carrying the tobacco to sell or for himself, the entry does not say. It says only that he would not give it up.

In the register the entry comes after one dated January 25, so the priest added it later, probably when the father came with the news. The date of January 18 may therefore be the day of death or the day the news reached Półrzeczki.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe content of the entry. Reading compared with the scan.
  • ProbableThe background: the tobacco monopoly in the Austrian lands from 1784, and its extension to Hungary only in 1850.
  • PossibleThat Franciszek was carrying tobacco across the border to sell. The entry speaks only of tobacco he would not give up.
  • To be checkedThe name of the pastor from Krościenko, which could not be read.

What we don’t know

  • Exactly where and on what day Franciszek was killed. The record of his burial should be in the registers of Krościenko parish.
  • Whether anyone answered for that shot.
