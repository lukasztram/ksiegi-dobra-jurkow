Jurków · 1773–1915 · from the publication “Trzy stulecia Jurkowa” (Three Centuries of Jurków)

Married Again Twenty Years Later

Tomasz and Agnieszka already had children when it came to light that they were related. The bishop ruled that they had married in good faith and ordered them to take their vows again.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

The Church forbade marriage between relatives up to the fourth degree, that is, down to the descendants of common great-great-grandparents. In a small village where people had married their neighbors for generations, such kinship was easy to come by, and it was not always remembered. For relatives to marry, a dispensation was needed, that is, a release from the prohibition.

In March 1774 the parish priest of Dobra, Father Sebastian Mikołajski, entered an unusual record in the marriage register. Tomasz Michałek and Agnieszka, recorded as Kulizonka, both of Jurków, were not marrying for the first time. They had married almost twenty years earlier and already had children when it came to light that they were related. In the eyes of church law, their marriage was therefore invalid.

Below the record the priest copied the dispensation of the auxiliary bishop Franciszek Potkański, issued in Kraków on March 3, 1774. The bishop found that Tomasz and Agnieszka had married in good faith, “ignorantes,” unaware of their kinship. He ordered the marriage renewed with a dispensation.

A year earlier, in February 1773, Jan Chalcarz of Dobra married Katarzyna Smoleń of Jurków. They were related in the third and fourth degrees. Their dispensation came all the way from Rome, dated December 7, 1769, and also covered a second couple, from Wilczyce and Półrzeczki. More than three years passed between the issuing of the dispensation and the wedding.

The Jurków registers record six marriages with a dispensation: in 1773, 1774, 1884, 1887, 1895, and 1915. The last is the marriage of my great-grandparents, Józef and Kunegunda Podgórny. Both descended from the same couple of house no. 5, who appear in the story “A Count at the Font”. It was the only wedding in Jurków in the war year of 1915.

In the 19th century one more consent was needed for a wedding. Under Austrian law, a young person under 24 needed the father’s permission. From the 1840s such consents were entered in the register in Polish. How certain is this

  • CertainThe records of the marriages with a dispensation, and their dates.
  • To be checkedThe full text of the copied dispensations of 1773 and 1774 has been only partly transcribed.

What we don’t know

  • How the kinship of Tomasz and Agnieszka came to light after twenty years of marriage.
  • How much the dispensation from Rome cost and who paid for it.
