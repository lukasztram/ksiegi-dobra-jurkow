Mszana Górna, Mszana Dolna parish · 1895–1906 · a story from my family

Great-Grandmother from Number 25

In the 1890s a widow with four grown children lived at number 25 in Mszana Górna. One of them was my great-grandmother Anna.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

My great-grandmother Anna Węglarz came from Mszana Górna, from house no. 25. Mszana Górna lies just beyond the western boundary of Dobra parish, and at that time it was divided between two parishes: one part belonged to Mszana Dolna, the other to Niedźwiedź. House no. 25 stood in the Mszana part, so its residents were recorded in the registers of Mszana Dolna. Annual copies of these registers survive for 1890–1916. In them one can follow a single house for a dozen or so years.

A widow and four grown children

In the 1890s the farm at no. 25 was run by a widow, Wiktoria Węglarz, née Kosmal, born December 5, 1842. Her husband Wojciech had died before 1890, since his death does not appear in the annual copies. Four grown children lived with their mother: Joanna, Jan, Anna, and Stefan.

We know their birth dates thanks to a custom that appears in these registers in the mid-1890s. At a child’s baptism the priest added three dates in the last column, with the abbreviations “c.,” “p.,” and “m.” At the baptism of Wiktoria’s granddaughter in 1899 it reads: “c. 19/2 895; p. 3/12 864; m. 22/1 869.” The first date is the wedding day of the child’s parents, and it matches the marriage register to the day. The other two, then, are the birth dates of the father and the mother. So Jan Węglarz was born on December 3, 1864.

Three weddings

Jan was the first to marry. On February 19, 1895, at the age of 30 (the priest wrote 29), he married Anna Kuczaj from Łostówka, from house no. 60. The wedding was recorded in the Łostówka section, because the bride came from there, and it was blessed by the curate from Limanowa, Father Wojciech Zarański. Anna moved to her husband’s home at no. 25.

A year later, on February 8, 1896, the eldest of the siblings, Joanna, married. She was 33. Her husband, Jan Dudzik from no. 29, was 25 and a reservist, meaning he had already done his service in the emperor’s army (that service is the subject of the story “The Emperor’s Soldiers”). The bride’s brother was a witness. Joanna moved just four house numbers away.

Anna, my great-grandmother, stayed at home the longest. On June 1, 1899, she was godmother to her brother’s daughter, who was named after her grandmother: Wiktoria. The priest recorded the godmother as “Anna Węglarz virgo,” that is, unmarried. She was 28 at the time.

She did not marry until October 21, 1903, at 33, the same age as her sister before her.

The groom was a widower from a neighboring village. The witnesses were Jan Jania and Anna’s brother-in-law, Jan Dudzik. The priest first wrote the surname of the groom’s first wife as “Piwowarczyk” and then crossed out the ending.

Births and funerals

In November 1895 Jan and Anna, née Kuczaj, had their first daughter. She was named Joanna, and her godmother was the aunt whose name she inherited.

On April 3, 1897, at no. 29, Joanna and Jan Dudzik’s first child, Aniela, was born. In baptisms from this part of the village the midwife column usually shows Anna Starmach or Regina Węglarz. This time the priest wrote: “Victoria Kosmal.” That was the maiden name of the child’s grandmother.

Half a year later, on October 12, 1897, Anna, née Kuczaj, gave birth to a son. The baby was so weak that no one waited for the priest.

A baptism “ex aqua,” with water, is one given in urgent need by someone from the household, most often by the midwife. The boy died the same day. The death register gives “debilitas naturalis,” congenital weakness. He was probably named after his uncle.

Uncle Stefan, the youngest of the four siblings, outlived his nephew by less than three years. He died at no. 25 on June 19, 1900. He was 25. The priest wrote the cause in Polish: “zapalenie płuc,” pneumonia.

To Podobin

After the wedding Anna moved to her husband’s home in Podobin, house no. 77. Podobin belonged to the parish of Niedźwiedź, so my great-grandmother no longer appears in the Mszana registers. From the family tree I know that a year later, in October 1904, her first son, Franciszek, my grandfather, was born there.

Wiktoria Węglarz died at no. 25 on April 15, 1906. She was 63; the priest wrote 64. As the cause he gave “ból żołądka,” stomach pain, and in the remarks column he added her date of birth. Jan, with his wife and children, remained in the house.

Eleven years and ten entries: that is what the registers say about house no. 25. The name Węglarz appears in them in one more place, a distant one. In December 1894, twenty-seven-year-old Maciej Węglarz from Mszana Górna died in Budapest. He and others who left to find work are the subject of the story “To Pest and Chicago”.

How certain is this

  • CertainThree weddings, four baptisms, and three deaths at nos. 25 and 29, with dates, ages, and witnesses. I compared every entry with the scan.
  • ProbableThat the abbreviations “p.” and “m.” at baptisms mark the birth dates of the father and the mother. The date next to “c.” matches the marriage register to the day, and the ages calculated from the other two dates differ from the ages recorded at the wedding by a year at most.
  • PossibleThat the “Victoria Kosmal” recorded as midwife in 1897 was the child’s grandmother. It could also have been a relative of hers with the same first name and surname.
  • To be checkedMy grandfather’s date of birth comes from the family tree, not from the registers. The first name of Wiktoria’s father in her death record is hard to read (Szymon?).

What we don’t know

  • When Wojciech Węglarz, Wiktoria’s husband, died. His death is not in the annual copies from 1890–1912.
  • Whether Maciej Węglarz, who died in Budapest, was related to the family at no. 25.
  • When Kazimierz Tram’s first wife died, and what Anna’s life in Podobin was like. The registers of Niedźwiedź parish from those years are not online.
  • Where exactly house no. 25 stood.
