Chyszówki and Półrzeczki · 1916 · from the publication “Chyszówki w księgach zmarłych” (Chyszówki in the Death Registers)

A New Cemetery without the Parish Priest’s Consent

In 1916 a priest from Jurków began burying the dead of Chyszówki in a new, unconsecrated cemetery. It left a protest in the register, complete with an exclamation mark.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

For centuries Jurków belonged to the parish of Dobra. The village did not build its own church until 1913, and it became a separate parish only in 1925. Before that happened, the death registers recorded a dispute over who was to bury the dead from the surrounding villages.

On January 28, 1916, six-month-old Stanisława Drzyzga, daughter of Jan and Marianna, died in Chyszówki. Below her entry, the priest who kept the register wrote across the whole width of the page, with an exclamation mark at the end:

For the next person who died, the priest wrote more briefly: “Sepelivit eodem modo ut supra,” buried in the same manner as above. By early 1917 the cooperator from Jurków had buried a dozen or so of the dead from Chyszówki this way. In August 1916 the same note appears in the register of Półrzeczki, next to the 63-year-old widow Marianna Bolisęga.

A cooperator was an assistant priest. Father Jan Nagórzański—for that is how published accounts and other entries in the registers give his name—worked in Jurków, which already had a church but was not yet a parish. By burying the dead in the new cemetery in Jurków, he was doing what belonged to the parish priest in Dobra.

A few years later the matter was settled: in 1925 Jurków became a separate parish, and Chyszówki belongs to it to this day. The last entry in this death register dates from December 1926.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe wording of the 1916 notes in the registers of Chyszówki and Półrzeczki. The transcriptions were checked against the scans.
  • CertainIn the note of January 1916 the cooperator’s name is written “Nagorański,” without the “z.” It was written the same way at the burials from Półrzeczki. The transcription was checked against the scan (as of November 2026).
  • ProbableThe correct form of the name is Nagórzański. This is how histories of the parish of Jurków give it, and at baptisms in Łostówka in 1916 it was written “Nagorzański.”

What we don’t know

  • Who established the new cemetery in Jurków and when it was consecrated.
  • How the people of Chyszówki saw the dispute, and whether they themselves asked to be buried in Jurków, closer to home.
