Mszana Dolna parish · 1894–1912 · a new story from the registers of a neighboring parish

Forest, Sawmill, and Water

Of some 3,500 burials from 1897–1912, at least thirty-five were accidents: in the forest, in the sawmill, in the water, and by fire.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

This story, like “To Pest and Chicago”, comes from the registers of the neighboring parish of Mszana Dolna. Until 1896 the priests there wrote a single word next to most of the dead: “naturalis,” natural death. From 1897 on they gave the cause, usually in Polish and as they heard it from the family. Thanks to this, for 1897–1912 we know not only who died of pneumonia or of old age. We also know who was killed by a tree, who by a cow, and who by lightning.

There are at least thirty-five such entries among three and a half thousand deaths. That is one death in a hundred.

The forest, the saw, and the cart

Forest work was done in winter, when timber is easier to drag out over the snow. On January 24, 1906, Jan Klimek of Łętowe, a farmer from house no. 53, was killed. He was 44 and left a wife, Maria, née Pajdo.

Half a year later in the same village, at number 61, fifty-two-year-old Józef Pajdo died: “wozem ciężarowym przejechany,” run over by a heavy wagon. So the same page of the register holds two such accidents from one year and one village.

Józef Wydra of Kasinka Mała, aged 50, was killed on August 25, 1909. The priest wrote: “zabity w traku,” killed in the “trak.” A trak is the frame saw in a sawmill that cuts logs into boards. Sawmills and the people who made their living from the forest are the subject of the stories “People of the Forest” and “The Railroad and the Steam Saw”.

The shortest of these entries concerns Jan Jackowiec of Łostówka, a forty-year-old farmer from house no. 101. He died on June 7, 1900. In the cause column stand four words: “w lesie go zabito,” he was killed in the forest. Whether he was killed by a tree during logging or by people, the register does not say.

Wojciech Majeran of Mszana Górna, aged 28, was killed in January 1899: “spadł z ławy,” he fell from the “ława.” A ława is a bench, but in the highlanders’ dialect it is also a footbridge over a stream.

Water

In 1900–1912 twelve people drowned in the parish. Six of them were children, from an infant to a nine-year-old. Two children drowned in a well.

Eight of the twelve drownings fall in the months from March to June, when the Raba, the Mszanka, and the streams carry the most water. In July 1900 two young people drowned within five days: sixteen-year-old Katarzyna Płoskonka of Kasinka Mała and twenty-two-year-old Sebastian Knapczyk of Mszana Górna. Next to his name the priest recorded how it happened: an epileptic seizure threw him into the water.

How water took people in the parish of Dobra is told in the story “Water”.

Fire

Nine people died of burns. Six of them were children aged two to twelve. The priests wrote it in various ways: “oparzenie,” “z poparzenia,” “oparzelisko” (a burn, from burns, a burn wound). In the cottages cooking was done over an open fire, and small children were left alone beside it.

Only once did the priest record a blaze. On March 25, 1904, in Kasinka Mała, at number 143, a twenty-six-year-old woman died: “burned to death in a fire.”

Lightning, a cow, and a snake

Lightning killed two men in the parish. On June 10, 1896, Andrzej Szczypka of Mszana Dolna, aged 39, was killed. On May 2, 1901, Jan Banik of Łostówka, a farmer from house no. 62, aged 51, was killed. For both, the priest used the same Latin words: “fulmine ictus,” struck by lightning. Similar entries from the parish of Dobra are collected in the story “One Bolt of Lightning”.

On September 16, 1901, ten-year-old Walenty Dziedzina was killed in Łętowe.

It was usually children who herded the cattle.

In September 1907 an eleven-year-old girl died in Glisne. The cause: “ukąszenie od węża,” a snakebite. In these mountains the only venomous snake is the adder. And in March 1898 a two-year-old child froze to death in Łostówka. The child was buried only after more than three weeks.

By human hand

Three entries speak of violence. On November 5, 1894, Jan Jantos of Mszana Górna, a twenty-three-year-old bachelor, was killed. The priest wrote one word: “occisio,” homicide. On December 3, 1902, in Łostówka, sixty-five-year-old Regina Chorągwicka died, and next to her name was written: “przez pobicie,” from a beating. In August 1907 in Mszana Dolna a visitor died of blood loss after being wounded; he is the subject of the story “The Circus Director”.

Twice, in 1891 and in 1897, the priest recorded a suicide.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe entries cited by name, with dates and causes of death. I checked each one against the scan.
  • ProbableThe numbers of drownings and burns: counted from the transcriptions, as of November 2026. I may have missed entries in which the cause was written in an unusual way. That the spring drownings are linked to high water.
  • PossibleThat the “ława” Wojciech Majeran fell from was a footbridge. That Jan Klimek was killed by a tree during logging: the word before “zabity drzewem” is illegible.
  • To be checkedIn the entry for Sebastian Knapczyk one word is uncertain (“wrzucony do wody,” thrown into the water?).

What we don’t know

  • Who killed Jan Jackowiec and Jan Jantos, and why, and whether anyone answered for it. The records of the court in Mszana Dolna or the newspapers of the time might tell.
  • How many accidents there were before 1897, when the priests did not record causes of death.
  • In which sawmill Józef Wydra was killed.
