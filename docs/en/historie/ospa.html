Smallpox and Vaccination

Smallpox returned every dozen years or so and killed mostly small children. From the 1830s on, the registers also show the other side of the story: vaccination.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

Smallpox came back to our villages every dozen years or so. For the dead, the priest wrote “variolae” or “in variolis,” of smallpox. Those who died of it were most often small children.

  • 180765 dead, all under 13, including 43 under 5. The most in Dobra, Porąbka, and Wilczyce.
  • 1810–181138 dead, all of them children. In 1810 the most in Półrzeczki, where 16 died.
  • 184720 dead, in the year of the famine.
  • 186013 dead, almost all children, in Półrzeczki, Chyszówki, Wilczyce, Zadziele, and Łostówka.
  • 186712 dead, almost all under 5.
  • 187348 dead, 37 of them under 5. The most in Wilczyce and Dobra. That year is the subject of the story “1873 in Zadziele”.
  • 188541 dead. Almost two-thirds were small children, but seven were between 15 and 21.

The numbers cover the death registers of all nine villages of the parish, as of November 2026. I counted the annual copies only for years for which the main registers are missing.

Vaccinated and unvaccinated

Vaccination against smallpox with cowpox was developed at the end of the 18th century by the English physician Edward Jenner. In our registers the first trace of vaccination appears in the 1830s, and from two sides at once. In 1837 in Jurków, for one-year-old Franciszka Kołodziej, the priest wrote: “in variolis non vaccin.,” of smallpox, not vaccinated. And four years later in Dobra, eleven-year-old Urban Miśkowiec died even though he had already been vaccinated.

“Impfung pro 842”

The baptism registers were also used for vaccination. In 1842, in the registers of Dobra, Jurków, and Chyszówki, someone in an unfamiliar hand, in German Kurrent script, wrote across the full width of the page below the entries, in German: “Impfung pro 842,” vaccination for the year 1842, and signed it. Such notes recur in later years: in 1845, 1847, 1850, and 1853 in Dobra; in 1857 with the date “Dobra am 28. November 857”; and in 1862 in Latin: “ad hoc vaccin. pro 862,” vaccinated up to this point for the year 1862. In 1865 in Chyszówki someone added in Polish: “Dr Trembecki szczepił,” Dr. Trembecki vaccinated.

The note most likely marked how far down the register the list of children to be vaccinated reached in a given year.

In Chyszówki in 1842 the note was placed below the baptism of Maciej of house no. 19, son of Walenty and Maria, born January 22. In April the priest entered him in the death register.

Vaccination did not put an end to smallpox at once. In 1873 it still killed 48 people, and in 1885, 41, including several teenagers and young adults. In Dobra, at house no. 199, two sisters, aged eighteen and ten, died that year within three weeks. After 1886, deaths from smallpox appear in the registers only now and then.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe notes “non vaccinatis” and “jam vaccinatus,” and the vaccination notes below the baptisms. Readings compared with the scans.
  • ProbableThat the boy who died on April 11, 1842, is the Maciej baptized on January 26: the same house no. 19, the father Walenty, and the age of two months. Also the meaning of the “Impfung pro…” notes.
  • To be checkedThe numbers of dead, as of November 2026, were counted automatically from the transcriptions and may be somewhat too low, because the priest did not always give the cause. The doctor’s name from 1865 and the signatures under the notes are uncertain readings.

What we don’t know

  • How many children were vaccinated, and who did the vaccinating, in the early years. The vaccination lists have not survived in the registers.
  • Whether anyone in the villages had already been vaccinated in 1807–1811.
