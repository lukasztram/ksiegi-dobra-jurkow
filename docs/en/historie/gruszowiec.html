Gruszowiec · 1786–1924 · a new story from the registers

Wolves in Gruszowiec

The registers of Gruszowiec were read in November 2026. The village’s oldest death register records a girl torn apart by wolves. Five days earlier the same fate befell a boy from neighboring Porąbka.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

In November 2026 the registers of Gruszowiec were transcribed: baptisms for 1786–1898, marriages for 1786–1924, and deaths for 1786–1875. That is almost three thousand entries, and more than five thousand counting the duplicate copies of the registers (as of November 2026). For a long time one of them had no counterpart in the transcribed registers of the parish. When the registers of Porąbka were transcribed, it turned out that the same summer wolves had killed another child.

Torn apart by wolves

On July 17, 1789, the parish priest recorded the death of thirteen-year-old Marianna Gąsior of house no. 16. In the column for the cause of death he wrote three Latin words.

The priest wrote nothing more: neither where it happened nor whether the girl was alone. It was midsummer, when children herded cattle in the clearings at the edge of the forest, but whether that was the case this time, we do not know.

Five days earlier in Porąbka

On July 12, 1789, five days before Marianna’s death, the priest entered in the death register of Porąbka seven-year-old Paweł, son of Jan Stokłosa of house no. 18. The cause of death is almost the same.

Porąbka and Gruszowiec are neighboring villages. Two children killed within a single week were probably victims of the same pack, which that summer was coming close to the villages. There are no other such entries in the transcribed registers of the parish.

At that time wolves lived in the forests throughout the Carpathians. Attacks on people were rare, but they did happen, and children in particular were among the victims. The historian Dariusz Tarasiuk has described such cases from the mid-19th century in the Lublin Governorate.

A tree and a bridge

The forest and the water took people later as well. On April 7, 1831, next to a seventeen-year-old boy from house no. 1, the priest wrote: “arbore supresus,” crushed by a tree. Work at logging is the subject of the story “People of the Forest”.

On July 31, 1858, 31-year-old Mateusz, son of Franciszek Jordan, was killed: “de ponte lapsus occisus,” he fell from a bridge and was killed. The priest added: “cum scitu Officii C. R. Districtualis,” with the knowledge of the imperial-royal district office. Two years earlier, next to 32-year-old Wincenty Jania, who died suddenly, he noted that the district office in Tymbark knew of it. This was how the priest recorded, for sudden and accidental deaths, that the authorities knew about the case.

The year of hunger in Gruszowiec

In 1847, 60 people died in Gruszowiec, almost five times as many as in an ordinary year. On June 12 an unknown twenty-year-old beggar, a stranger from other parts, died in the house of Jan Miśkowiec.

He was buried by a Cistercian monk. That year in the other villages is the subject of the story “The Year of Hunger”. In 1855, during another time of destitution, the priest again wrote “ob miseriam,” of destitution, next to the dead of Gruszowiec.

What else is in the Gruszowiec registers

  • 1834The witnesses at the wedding of Stanisław Chalcarz and Anna Przybytek were the leaseholder of the estate, Józef Kisielewski, and the mandatary (the manor’s paid administrative official) Aleksander Garbiński. Both are the subject of the story “The Manor Official of Dobra”.
  • 1890Next to the baptism of Jan, son of Szymon Miśkowiec, someone added years later: “+ in America 1939,” died in America.
  • 1891Next to the baptism of Józef, son of Wojciech Mitek, a note was added about his marriage in Ostrava in 1926. Departures from the parish are the subject of the story “To Ostrava and Overseas”.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe quoted entries from Gruszowiec and Porąbka from 1789, 1831, 1834, 1847, 1856, 1858, 1890, and 1891, checked against the scans.
  • PossibleThat both children were killed by the same pack. The registers give only the date and the cause.
  • ProbableThe death counts (as of November 2026). The transcriptions of the Gruszowiec registers are recent and have not yet been checked in full, and many entries have fields that were read with uncertainty.
  • To be checkedThe surname of Józef Mitek’s wife in the note about the marriage in Ostrava.

What we don’t know

  • Where and how Marianna Gąsior and Paweł Stokłosa died, and whether anyone was with them.
  • On which bridge Mateusz Jordan was killed.
