Jurków and Chyszówki · 1901–1912 · from the publications on Jurków and Chyszówki

Sipka

In the early 20th century, the priest wrote a word from the local dialect beside the names of children who died. Today it needs explaining.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

Priests usually recorded causes of death in Latin. Yet in 1901–1912 the death register of Jurków contains, fourteen times, a word that is in no Latin dictionary: “sipka.” The priest probably wrote down the word the parents used.

In the dialect of the Zagórzanie, the highlanders of this area, sipka means hoarseness. The same word lies behind the Polish “sipły,” meaning hoarse. In a small child such hoarseness was no trifle. It meant a serious disease of the larynx, in which the child choked.

What disease it was, the priest himself explained. In 1901 diphtheria was raging in neighboring Chyszówki. Next to eleven-month-old Anna Gąsior he wrote “sipka (diphteritis),” and for five-year-old Szymon Janas he used both names at once.

In house no. 50 in Chyszówki diphtheria took two children of Jan Pałka and Katarzyna, née Węgrzyn: two-year-old Anna on January 30 and seven-year-old Michał on February 18, 1901. For Michał the priest used a second dialect word: “zawalnice (diphteritis).” Almost the same word, written “zawolnice,” appears in Jurków as early as 1819, for a six-year-old girl with a throat illness.

It was mostly infants who died of “sipka.” In Jurków eight of the fourteen children did not live to be a month old, and the oldest was seven. There was no diphtheria vaccine yet.

Words we do not yet understand

For several deaths in Jurków the priest wrote “dobrowolna” (in standard Polish, ‘voluntary’). In Chyszówki he wrote “moryszko” and “ryszka,” in Półrzeczki in 1904, for a one-month-old infant, “morzysko,” and in Chyszówki in 1905, for a three-year-old, “mordowanie brzucha” (literally ‘tormenting of the belly’). If anyone remembers these words from home, please let me know.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe meaning of the word “sipka.” The priest himself explained it in 1901, and it agrees with the dialect.
  • To be checkedThe words “moryszko,” “ryszka,” and “morzysko” deserve another look on the scans.

What we don’t know

  • What the words “dobrowolna,” “moryszko,” “ryszka,” and “morzysko” mean.
