Jurków, house no. 78 · 1873–1941 · a new story from the registers and archives

A Lawmaker and a Mayor from Jurków

Two sons of Sebastian and Rozalia Łaskuda of house no. 78 in Jurków left their mark on the history of the whole area. One sat in the parliaments in Lviv and Warsaw; the other led the Dobra municipality in its hardest years.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

Sebastian Łaskuda and Rozalia, née Fudalej, lived at house no. 78 in Jurków. They married in January 1871. Sebastian was the son of Jan, recorded in the register as a ⅛ kmieć, that is, a farmer on an eighth of an old full holding. Eight of their children were born at house no. 78. Two of the sons left their mark on the history of Jurków and of the whole municipality: the eldest, Michał, and the second-youngest, Szymon.

The future deputy’s godmother was most likely Kunegunda, née Przybytek, of house no. 2, the daughter of the manor’s forester and my great-great-great-grandmother. Her father is the subject of the story “The Forester of Dobra”, and the midwife Teresa Smoleń appears in the story “Midwives without Schooling”.

Deputy Michał Łaskuda

Michał was a farmer in Jurków. For many years up to 1914 he was the village head (“wójt”) and chairman of the supervisory board of the local Kasa Stefczyka, a cooperative savings and loan fund for peasants. In 1913–1914 he sat in the Diet of Galicia, the provincial parliament of Austrian Poland, in Lwów (now Lviv), as deputy for the Limanowa district. He belonged to the Polish People’s Party (PSL). During the First World War he served in the Austrian army, in the 20th Infantry Regiment, on the Italian and Russian fronts. The same regiment recurs in the parish registers from the 1830s on, as the story “The Emperor’s Soldiers” tells.

After Poland regained independence, he was elected in 1919 to the Legislative Sejm on the list of Wincenty Witos’s PSL “Piast.” He worked on the committees for public works and for water affairs. In 1922 he entered the Sejm of the first term from a constituency covering the Nowy Sącz, Limanowa, Bochnia, and Wieliczka districts. In 1923 he left “Piast” and was then active in the Peasant Union (Związek Chłopski), and from 1926 in the Peasant Party (Stronnictwo Chłopskie). He wrote for the weekly “Piast.” He died in Jurków on April 29, 1929, and was buried there.

His photograph is held in the “Parlamentarzyści” (Parliamentarians) database of the Sejm Library. We do not know who took it or on what terms it may be reproduced, so we do not copy it here.

Wójt Szymon Łaskuda

Szymon was born on September 17, 1885, also at house no. 78. In 1930 he ran for the Sejm on the list of the Nonpartisan Bloc for Cooperation with the Government, but did not win a seat. The newspaper “Głos Podhala” (Voice of Podhale) listed him in the last, twelfth place on the list: “Łaskuda Szymon, age 45, farmer, Jurków.”

When in 1934 the villages of Dobra, Jurków, Porąbka, Gruszowiec, Chyszówki, Półrzeczki, and Wilczyce were merged into a single collective municipality of Dobra, its first wójt was Szymon Łaskuda. He still held the post during the German occupation, when the wójt had to mediate between the occupiers and the residents.

In January 1941 the Gestapo arrested him in Jurków on a charge of belonging to an underground organization, the Union of Armed Action (Związek Czynu Zbrojnego). In the same case the parish priest of Dobra, Father Edward Wojtusiak, was imprisoned. Szymon Łaskuda died in the Buchenwald concentration camp on December 23, 1941, as prisoner number 5016.

Father Wojtusiak survived the war and was the parish priest of Dobra until 1980. He founded the parish museum and archive and wrote down the history of the parish. It is most likely from his article on the Saxon era (1697–1763, when Poland was ruled by kings from Saxony) that the stories “The Heaped Bushel” and “Forty-One Charges” come.

One more Łaskuda

Walenty Łaskuda, born January 5, 1893, also came from Jurków. A second lieutenant in the 11th Infantry Regiment, he was killed on August 8, 1920, near Dorohusk, in the war against the Bolsheviks. He was posthumously awarded the Silver Cross of the Virtuti Militari Order. Whether he was related to the deputy and the wójt could not be established.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe baptisms of Michał and Szymon and their parents’ wedding. Entries compared with the scans. Michał’s terms as a deputy, according to the Sejm Library. Szymon’s death in Buchenwald, according to the camp’s death register.
  • ProbableThat the godmother of 1873 was Kunegunda, née Przybytek, of house no. 2. That the author of the article on the Saxon era was the parish priest of Dobra, Father Edward Wojtusiak.
  • To be checkedThe exact day of Szymon’s arrest: local accounts give January 19 or around January 21, 1941.

What we don’t know

  • Exactly when Szymon became wójt, and whether he had earlier been wójt of Jurków alone.
  • How the brothers viewed their different political paths: Michał in the peasant movement, Szymon on the list of the government bloc.
  • How 2nd Lt. Walenty Łaskuda was connected to the family at house no. 78.
