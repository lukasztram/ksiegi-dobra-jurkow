A Hundred Years, Give or Take

The oldest people in the parish registers were over a hundred. But the ages of the dead were estimated by eye and often inflated.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

According to the registers, the oldest person in the parish was Zofia Stokłosionka from Porąbka. She died on March 21, 1791, at house no. 3, and the priest wrote her age clearly: 115 years. Her entry was transcribed only in November 2026, together with the whole death register of Porąbka.

Right after her comes Stanisław Stokłosa from Dobra. He died on February 4, 1825, and the priest clearly wrote his age: 113. Andrzej Przybytek, who died in 1807, was supposedly the same age, though here the priest put a question mark next to the number. Katarzyna Gdowicz from Jurków and Andrzej Łukasik from Gruszowiec supposedly died at 110, and Zofia Dudzik from Chyszówki at 105.

Was that really so? The priest usually recorded the age of the deceased from what the family told him, not from a baptismal record. The numbers themselves show it.

Age by guesswork

In 1779–1849, almost half of the ages recorded for adults who died are round numbers: 40, 50, 60, 70. If ages had been given exactly, only one in ten would have been round. The priest wrote sixty 371 times, but fifty-nine and sixty-one together only 50 times (as of November 2026). The ages of the very old were estimated, and, as the examples below show, often on the high side.

Checked in the registers

Sometimes this can be checked. Katarzyna Gdowicz from Jurków died in April 1845. According to the register she was 110. She had married in 1791, and the priest recorded then that the bride was 22. At her death she was therefore about 76, not 110.

Mateusz Mycoń from Dobra died in 1848 as an old man of 96.

Yet at his wedding to Rozalia, in 1817, he gave his age as 45. The baptism register also has a Mateusz, son of Wojciech and Łucja Mycoń of Dobra, baptized on September 16, 1770. Everything fits: Mateusz Mycoń lived to 77. His family or the priest added almost twenty years.

Zofia Stokłosionka cannot be checked. If she was 115, she would have been born around 1676, that is, before the oldest surviving register of the parish, begun in 1678 after the church fire. The priest did not even record the name of her father or her husband. The ending “-onka” tells us only that she was born into the Stokłosa family or married a Stokłosa.

Stanisław Stokłosa cannot be checked as precisely. If he was 113, he would have been born around 1712, and the baptism register for 1704–1719 has not survived. In 1734 a Stanisław, son of Jan and Katarzyna Stokłosa, was baptized in Dobra. If that was him, he lived to 90. That is a fine age too.

Counting came later

After 1850 there are far fewer round ages in the registers, and almost no one is said to have passed ninety. In 1779–1849 the priest recorded an age of 90 or more 96 times, and in 1850–1899 only 13 times (as of November 2026). Toward the end of the 19th century the exact date of birth began to be entered for the deceased.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe ages recorded at deaths and at weddings, and the 1770 baptism of Mateusz Mycoń. Entries compared with the scans.
  • ProbableThat the Mateusz Mycoń baptized in 1770 is the same man as in 1848. His age at his wedding fits this. The numbers in the chart and in the text were counted automatically from transcriptions of the registers of all nine villages, as of November 2026.
  • PossibleThat the Stanisław Stokłosa who died in 1825 is the boy baptized in 1734.

What we don’t know

  • Who really was the oldest person in the history of the parish.
  • Whether families inflated the ages of the old on purpose, or simply did not remember.
