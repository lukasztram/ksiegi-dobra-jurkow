Mszana Dolna parish · 1892–1904 · a new story from the registers of a neighboring parish

Scarlet Fever, Dysentery, Measles

In Mszana Dolna parish, about twenty people were buried in a typical month. In December 1898, fifty-nine were buried.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

This story comes from the registers of the neighboring parish of Mszana Dolna, which included Mszana Dolna, Mszana Górna, Kasinka Mała, Łętowe, Słomka, Glisne, and part of Łostówka. In 1890–1909 an average of 240 people a year were buried there, that is, twenty a month. Two years stand out from the rest: 1892 and 1900. In both there were three hundred funerals or more.

In those years, for most of the dead, the priest wrote only “naturalis” in the cause column. He did, however, record infectious diseases, so one can trace how they moved through the villages.

July 1892: scarlet fever in Kasinka

The first to die was Władysław Kotarba, a six-year-old from Kasinka Mała, house no. 43. That was on July 1, 1892. A day later his seven-day-old sister Anna died. By the end of July seventeen people in the parish had died of scarlet fever, and eleven more in August, almost all of them in Kasinka.

On July 26 three children died in Kasinka on a single day. Among them was thirteen-year-old Regina Cieżak of house no. 123.

A week later her twenty-year-old sister Katarzyna died, and on August 16 her thirteen-year-old brother Jan. The parents buried three children in three weeks.

In all of 1892 there were 117 funerals in Kasinka Mała. In the other years there were 62 on average.

The disease moved on. By the fall it was in Mszana Dolna, and in November and December it killed eight people in Glisne. At number 205 in Mszana Dolna, within eighteen days in March 1893, three daughters of Józef Potaczek and Anna, née Surówka, died: six-year-old Wiktoria, one-and-a-half-year-old Julianna, and four-year-old Felicja. In the summer in Łostówka, at number 68, three sons of Jan Kołodziej died: nineteen-year-old Piotr, seven-year-old Wojciech, and thirteen-year-old Sebastian.

The last death from scarlet fever was recorded in October 1893. In sixteen months seventy people died. Almost all were children, but four were between 19 and 28 years old.

August 1895: dysentery in Łostówka

Dysentery, which the priest wrote in Latin as “dissenteria,” is a disease of summer and dirty water. In 1895 it killed 39 people in the parish: the first on July 28, the last on November 17. Twenty of them lived in Łostówka, twelve in Kasinka.

In August 1895, 42 people were buried in the parish, twice the usual number. Most of the dead were children, but in Łostówka five adults also died, aged 25 to 60.

December 1898: measles

Measles arrived in mid-November 1898 and lasted until March. It killed forty children, almost all of them under five.

In December 1898, 59 people were buried in the parish, the most of any month in those twenty years. On December 17 in Łętowe, at number 6, two children of Jakub Kuczaj died on the same day: Franciszek, who was two and a half, and six-month-old Anna. The priest joined the two entries with a bracket and added that he had buried them together.

1900: everything at once

The most funerals, 309, were in 1900. This time not one disease came, but several. From January to April children died of whooping cough, in March and April of scarlet fever. Throughout the year a throat disease was recorded which the priest called “angina” and the people “sipka,” “sipek,” or “zawalnica.” Thirty-six people died of it, half of them in Kasinka.

Sipka is diphtheria. The same word in the death register of Jurków is described in the story “Sipka”. In 1902 diphtheria killed 44 people in the parish of Mszana Dolna, and the register then shows a difference between the small town and the villages. In Mszana Dolna the cause was written in Latin, the language of a doctor: “croup,” “laryngitis crouposa difteritica.” In the villages it says “sipek,” “sipka,” and “zawalnica.”

A remedy already existed by then. From 1894 Odo Bujwid was producing antidiphtheria serum in Kraków. At St. Louis Hospital in Kraków, mortality among children with diphtheria fell from 62 percent in 1885 to 22 percent in 1895. In the villages near Mszana children went on dying: the most deaths were recorded in precisely those years, 1900 and 1902.

A father and two daughters

In the spring of 1903 scarlet fever returned to Mszana Dolna. At number 193, two-year-old Tekla Dudzik died on May 26. Two days later her five-year-old sister Anna died, and on the same day their father.

Father and daughter were buried on the same day, May 30.

January 1904: whooping cough in Łętowe

Between January 8 and March 2, 1904, fourteen infants died of whooping cough in Łętowe. The oldest was thirteen months old. In the whole year there were 29 funerals in Łętowe, so every second one was a child taken by whooping cough.

In the parish of Dobra the same diseases are described in the stories “Smallpox and Vaccination”, “The First Year of Life”, and “The Spanish Flu”.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe entries quoted in the boxes and the deaths of the Kuczaj children, the Dudzik family, and the Potaczek sisters: these I checked against the scan. The other names and dates come from the transcription.
  • ProbableThe numbers of deaths from particular diseases: counted from the transcriptions of all the annual copies, as of November 2026. The real numbers were probably higher, because until 1896 the priest gave no cause for most of the dead.
  • PossibleThat the Latin diagnoses in Mszana Dolna came from a doctor who examined the dead in the town but not in the villages.
  • To be checkedThe ages of some of the children from 1900: in that year’s volume the figures stand between the columns for years, months, and days.

What we don’t know

  • Where the scarlet fever came to Kasinka Mała from in the summer of 1892.
  • Whether a doctor was called to the sick, whether the serum reached the countryside, and whether anyone closed the schools.
  • What happened after 1909: from 1910 on, Kasinka Mała is missing from the annual copies, and after 1912 there are no copies of the death registers at all.
