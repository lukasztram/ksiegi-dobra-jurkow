Jurków, house no. 108 · 1892–1953 · from my book “Ród Podgórnich z Jurkowa” (The Podgórni Family of Jurków)

Father Leon of Jurków

Andrzej Podgórni of house no. 108 was a soldier of the Polish Legions, a Dominican, and a student in Belgium. The war found him in monasteries in the Kresy, the eastern borderlands of prewar Poland.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

In the story about the chapel at Szyrszenie I looked for the priest from the Podgórni family whose father is said to have built the chapel for him in 1826. I did not find that priest. But there was a priest in the family, only a hundred years later. He was Father Leon, a Dominican, and I can tell his story thanks to the parish registers and one scholarly article.

House no. 108

Andrzej Podgórni was born on December 19, 1892, in Jurków, at house no. 108, and was baptized that same day. He was the son of Antoni Podgórni and Anna, née Chalcarz. His godparents were Michał and Barbara Szyrszeń, and the midwife at the birth was Katarzyna Podgórni.

His grandfather Jan was the son of Melchior and Anna, née Styś, of house no. 5, the one opposite the chapel. Melchior was the brother of my ancestor Józef. Father Leon and my great-grandfather Józef therefore shared great-great-grandparents, Jan and Katarzyna Podgórni.

In November 1846 Jan, a twenty-eight-year-old bachelor, married nineteen-year-old Katarzyna Jania of house no. 45, at house no. 108. Under the law of the time the bride was a minor, so her father entered his consent in the register, in Polish.

Below it the priest also noted the consent of the Dobra dominium, the manorial estate, which oversaw the affairs of minors. Eight months earlier the manor had lost its official, as the story “The Manor Official of Dobra” tells.

Soldier and friar

Before entering the order, Andrzej was a soldier for four years: first in the Polish Legions, then in the Austrian army, and finally in the Polish Army. On November 11, 1919, the first anniversary of Poland’s regained independence, he entered the Dominican order in Kraków and took the name Leon. A year later he made his first religious vows. He studied philosophy at the order’s house of studies in Lwów (now Lviv), and then in Leuven, Belgium. He was ordained a priest on August 2, 1925.

In the eastern borderlands

He worked in Dominican friaries in what was then eastern Poland, among them Jezupol (1937–1941) and Bohorodczany (1941–1942) near Stanisławów. From 1942 he was at Podkamień near Brody, in the monastery on the hill. From 1943 Poles fleeing the massacres in Volhynia took refuge there. By the end of that year there were about two thousand of them.

March 1944

On March 12–16, 1944, the monastery and the small town were attacked by a unit of Ukrainian nationalists from the UPA, supported by a Ukrainian SS police regiment under German command. Between 400 and 600 Poles were killed.

Father Leon survived. On March 17 he reached the Dominican friary in Tarnobrzeg. The chronicle of that friary recorded that he had arrived from Podkamień, “miraculously escaping death.” The next day he wrote a letter to the provincial describing the attack. At the end he added briefly: “I will now be in Tarnobrzeg; I kindly ask for an assignment,” that is, a posting to that friary.

After the war

He stayed in Tarnobrzeg until 1948. After that he was in friaries in Kraków, Prudnik, and Lublin, and from 1952 in Gidle. He died there on July 2, 1953, at the age of 60.

That first priest

The regional historian who wrote down the tradition about the chapel knew of Father Leon but concluded that he was not the priest in question, because the chapel is a hundred years older. He is right. The priest from the time the chapel was built is still waiting to be found, and Father Leon is the only priest from our family I know of for certain.

How certain is this

  • CertainAndrzej’s baptism in 1892 and his grandparents’ wedding in 1846, compared with the scans. His life in the order according to his personnel file in the Archive of the Polish Province of the Dominicans, as published by Katarzyna Matyja.
  • ProbableThat Melchior, Jan’s father, was the brother of my ancestor Józef of house no. 5, and therefore that Father Leon and my great-grandfather shared great-great-grandparents.
  • To be checkedIndividual words of Mikołaj Jania’s Polish consent of 1846. The handwriting is dense and unclear in places.

What we don’t know

  • How Father Leon survived the attack. His letter of March 1944 is in the Dominican archive and has not been published.
  • Whether he visited Jurków after entering the order, and whether the family remembered him.
  • Where he is buried.
