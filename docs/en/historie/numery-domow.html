What a House Number Tells

From 1779 on, the priest recorded the house number at every baptism, wedding, and funeral. These numbers tell how the village grew, how cottages were divided, and what happened to a number when a house disappeared.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

Anyone who looks for ancestors in the registers of Dobra parish quickly gets used to the number next to every entry: Jurków 5, Dobra 72, Półrzeczki 96. It is easy to take it for an address like today’s. But it is something else, and these numbers say more about the village than one might think.

A number from the emperor

After the First Partition of Poland in 1772, Dobra and Jurków found themselves in Galicia, under Austrian rule. The Austrians registered people and houses above all to know whom they could take into the army, and also for taxes. Such a census was called a conscription, and the number each house received in it was its conscription number. How the boys of the parish were later taken into the army is told in the story “The Emperor’s Soldiers”.

House numbers appear in the Dobra registers in August 1779. That is when the priest begins a new form, with a separate column for the house number.

The first entry with a number is the baptism of Regina, daughter of Mikołaj Małudy and Agnieszka from Jurków, on August 25, 1779: house no. 50.

The number is a sequence, not a place

Houses were numbered in order, and each new house received the first free number, regardless of where it stood. That is why the numbers of neighboring houses can differ greatly. At Trzopki in Jurków, house no. 137 stands right next to house no. 2: no. 2 is in the registers from the 18th century, while numbers in the 130s appear only in the 1880s.

The registers show how the houses in Jurków multiplied. For more than 60 years the numbers end at around 76–80. Then suddenly more than thirty are added, and after that they grow slowly: to 124 in the 1860s and to 141 around 1900. Today house numbers in Jurków go above 370.

1845: new numbers across the parish

This sudden jump was not just in Jurków. Between the summer of 1845 and early 1846, new, higher numbers appear in the registers of almost all the villages of the parish at once. This is how the highest house number recorded in the registers changed:

The table gives the highest house number recorded at least twice in a given year in the main registers of the parish; a single entry could have been a mistake. In Wilczyce the numbers hardly grew at all.

So many houses were not built in a single year, and right before the famine of 1847 at that, the subject of the story “The Year of Hunger”. The old numbers stayed where they were: the Podgórni family still lived at no. 5, the Gdowicz family at nos. 10 and 11. The new numbers went to families who had already been living in the village, only under someone else’s number: the Smoleń, Miśkowiec, Michałek, and Jania families.

It can be seen another way, too. In 1836–1844 in Jurków, at every other house number (51%), children were baptized whose fathers had at least two different surnames. In 1847–1855 this was true of only one number in five (22%). In Chyszówki this share fell from 59 to 14%, in Dobra from 42 to 20%. So it looks as though in 1845, during some revision of the census, cottages that until then had shared a number with a neighboring farmstead got numbers of their own. I have not found a document that would confirm this.

The number stays with the house

The number belonged to the house, not to the family. House no. 5 in Jurków was the home of my ancestors, the Podgórni family, from 1784 to 1926; the story “The Chapel at Szyrszenie” is about this place. In 1786–1812 house no. 2 was the home of the Czopek family. The Podgórni family appears there only in 1856, after Antoni Podgórni married Kunegunda Przybytek.

In the 1870s and 1880s, Józef Rapacz and his wife Kunegunda, née Lach, lived at no. 42 in Jurków. Their daughter Jadwiga was born here on October 15, 1877. Józef died in 1891; Kunegunda married again, to Szymon Paruźnik, and died at the same number in 1900. From 1902 it is Jadwiga who lives at no. 42, with her husband, Józef Niedośpiał. Through the daughter the house passed to another family, and the number stayed. The Niedośpiał family was still living at no. 42 in the second half of the 20th century.

Two houses numbered 123

Number 123 first appears in the Jurków registers in 1872. Living there was Józef Gdowicz, a lodger, from the family that had lived at nos. 10 and 11 since numbering began. A lodger (“komornik”) was a peasant without land of his own, but Józef already lived under a number of his own. In 1872 his wife Zofia, née Chalcarz, died, and a year later Józef married for the second time at this number.

He was 56 at the time, and his second wife, Franciszka Staniszewska from Chyszówki, was 46. Józef died in 1876 of pneumonia. In 1879 his daughter Marianna married at no. 123, and her children were born there in 1880–1886. The last time number 123 appears in the transcribed registers is in 1913, at the baptism of a son of Józef Ogryzek and Katarzyna, née Gąsior, cottagers.

Today number 123 in Jurków belongs to a house built around 1970. I do not know whether it stands where the Gdowicz cottage stood. But this is exactly how conscription numbers worked: when an old house disappeared, its free number could be given to a new house, even in a completely different part of the village. So number 123 in the registers and number 123 today are most likely two different houses.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe form with a house-number column from August 1779, and the entries about houses no. 42 and 123, compared with the scans. The sudden increase in numbers in 1845–1846 in the registers of the whole parish, counted from transcriptions of all the registers.
  • ProbableThat a new house received the first free number, and that numbers freed when a house disappeared were given out elsewhere. This is how conscription numbers in the Austrian lands are described, and the parish registers do not contradict it. That in 1845 cottages which had earlier shared a number with their neighbors got numbers of their own.
  • PossibleThat the house built around 1970 received number 123 after the Gdowicz cottage disappeared.
  • To be checkedThe last digit of the year in the heading of the 1779 form is hard to read; it agrees with the heading “Annus 1780” a few pages later. The house number in the death records of Zofia and Józef Gdowicz (1872 and 1876) is hard to read.

What we don’t know

  • Who assigned the new numbers in 1845, and on what basis.
  • When cottage no. 123 disappeared and where it stood. The Jurków registers after 1913 have survived only in part.
  • Whether at least some of the houses in Jurków were renumbered in the 20th century, and who assigned numbers after the war.
