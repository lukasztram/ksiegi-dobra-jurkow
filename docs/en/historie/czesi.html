Dobra, Jurków, Chyszówki · 1854–1904 · a new story from the registers

Czechs and Moravians

In the second half of the 19th century, people from Bohemia and Moravia came to the parish to work: officials, foresters, gardeners, railroad builders.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

From the mid-19th century on, people “ex Bohemia” and “e Moravia,” from Bohemia and from Moravia, appear more and more often in the parish registers. At the time they were all subjects of the same emperor. They came to work: to manage the estate, to guard the forests, to build roads and the railroad.

  • 1854Jan Klein, an imperial-royal road master from Červený Kostelec in Bohemia, aged 36, was married in Dobra.
  • 1862Franciszek Weigel from Bohemia, a widower aged 45 and steward of the manor in Dobra, married here for the second time.
  • 1871Antoni Hüttel, a forester and the son of parents from Bohemia, had a daughter baptized in Dobra.
  • 1877Wacław Kubik, a gardener from Bohemia, aged 24, married Anna Horáček, the daughter of a tailor from Přelouč. They lived in house no. 1, which most likely means the manor.
  • 1884–1885Jan Krusch, a forest ranger from Moravia, married in Jurków and died there a year later, aged 24.
  • 1885Józef Janota from Lomnice in Bohemia, a supervisor on the railroad construction, was married in Dobra.
  • 1894–1904The Vaněk family from Moravia had seven children baptized in Dobra.

The foresters are the subject of the story “People of the Forest”, the railroad builders of “The Railroad and the Steam Saw”, and the Vaněk family of “The Innkeeper’s Grandson”.

The gelder from Hrozenkov

Not everyone came to stay. In April 1881, Franciszek Staroba, a 31-year-old bachelor from Moravia, died suddenly on the road in Jurków. He was an itinerant gelder: he went from village to village castrating farm animals.

And the other way

People from the parish also went to Moravia, to the mines in Ostrava. Next to several baptisms from Dobra, the priest added years later that the person baptized had died “in Ostrava Moravia,” in Ostrava in Moravia. This is the subject of the story “To Ostrava and Overseas”.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe entries quoted here. The most important ones were checked against the scans.
  • To be checkedThe spelling of some place names from Bohemia, which the priest wrote down by ear, for example the home villages of Weigel and Kubik.

What we don’t know

  • How many newcomers from Bohemia and Moravia lived in the parish at any one time. The registers record only those who married here, had children baptized, or died.
  • Which of them stayed for good and which went back home.

Sources

  • Marriage registers of Dobra, ref. 4268/4520 (scans 008, 015, 034, 042); of Jurków, ref. 4268/4530 (scan 050); baptism registers of Dobra, ref. 4268/3545 (scans 017, 084–124); death register of Jurków, ref. 4268/5525 (scans 025, 028); on the Metryki website of the Polish Genealogical Society (PTG).
