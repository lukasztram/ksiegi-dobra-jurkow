The Monks of Szczyrzyc

The Cistercian abbey of Szczyrzyc stands a dozen or so kilometers from Dobra. For more than two hundred years, its monks came to the parish when priests were lacking, when famine reigned, and when cholera came.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

The Cistercian monastery was founded in 1234 in Ludźmierz, in the Podhale region. Around 1243 the monks moved to Szczyrzyc, a dozen or so kilometers northwest of Dobra. It is the only Cistercian monastery in the Polish lands that has existed without interruption since its founding.

The Cistercians were neighbors of the Dobra estate. According to Father Edward Wojtusiak, in the 18th century their lands reached from the north as far as Porąbka, where they had a manor farm with a brewery. They quarreled with the manor in Dobra over the boundary with Stróża, and in 1740–1753 the monastery’s estate manager, Father Jacek Wolański, brought complaints before the court on its behalf. How a different lord left his mark in the registers of Porąbka is told in the story “Porąbka, Another Lord’s Village”.

Standing in for the parish priest

The oldest trace of the Cistercians in the parish registers dates from 1683. The parish priest was away, and a child from Chyszówki was baptized by a monk whom he had left as his substitute.

In the early 19th century a Cistercian was the curate in Dobra. In September 1820 Sylwester Gonda, recorded as “Ord. Cist.” and “Vicarius in Dobra,” stood godfather to a girl from Dobra. He died in Dobra on April 15, 1831, in house no. 122, at the age of 54. The priest recorded him as “Presbiter Regularis Ordinis Cisterciensium,” a religious priest of the Cistercian order.

During the Partitions of Poland the monastery lost its land, and the authorities forbade it to accept novices. They did, however, allow it to run a four-grade school for village children, which the writer Władysław Orkan attended. The Dobra registers show when the monks were needed here most.

Famine

In 1843 baptisms in Gruszowiec were performed by Bernard Jankowski, “e conventu Szczyrzycensi,” from the Szczyrzyc convent. He came back to the parish in the year of the famine. In June and July 1847 he buried at least 137 people from all nine villages of the parish: the most from Chyszówki (29) and Dobra (23), followed by Półrzeczki, Jurków, Porąbka, and Wilczyce. For eighteen of them he wrote “ob famem,” of hunger (as of November 2026). In some of the registers his surname was at first read as “Dankowski,” which is why we previously knew only of the burials from Chyszówki and Dobra.

A week later he buried, in Chyszówki, a beggar whose name was unknown, a newcomer who had died of hunger in Paweł Jania’s field. That summer is the subject of the story “The Year of Hunger”.

Cholera

Eight years later cholera came. In the summer of 1855 the dead of the parish were buried by a Cistercian, Father Maksymilian Klemp. In July and August he buried 42 people: ten each from Jurków and Półrzeczki, nine from Dobra, four each from Chyszówki and Wilczyce, two each from Gruszowiec and Zadziele, and one from Łostówka. Cholera was recorded for 31 of them (as of November 2026). Among them was fourteen-year-old Marianna, the daughter of Błażej Wątor, a lodger from Dobra. That same summer a doctor sent to fight the epidemic died in Dobra; the story “The Doctor from Vienna” is about him.

The death of the parish priest

From the early 1840s the parish was led by the parish priest Jakub Jana. He died in Dobra on March 2, 1871. The last sacraments were given to him by the Cistercian prior.

He was buried by the dean from Tymbark, and the prior was also present at the funeral. After that the parish was run by the curate Aleksander Bogusz. From April to July 1871 he was helped by a Cistercian, Father Roman Kurzeja, “presbyter ex Ordine Cisterciensi.” Father Kurzeja baptized about 30 children, buried 11 of the dead, and blessed 7 marriages, in all the villages of the parish (as of November 2026). From June on, Bogusz was already signing as parish priest.

Into the 20th century

  • 1875Cistercians from Szczyrzyc came to the funeral of Father Ludwik Rudnicki, the organist’s son, and Father Maksymilian preached the sermon. The story “The Organists of Dobra” tells about it.
  • 1890Father Eustachy, recorded as “cistersus,” baptized and buried the dead. His surname was written in the registers so illegibly that we cannot read it.
  • 1896Father Wincenty Kolor, “Cistersio e Szczyrzyc,” buried an 87-year-old woman from Dobra. The prior who prepared the parish priest for death in 1871 bore the same name.
  • 1917Father Dominik Jurkowski, “ex Ord. Cisterciensium,” blessed the marriage of a 65-year-old widower and a widow in Półrzeczki.
  • 1927A wedding in Dobra was blessed by the prior of the Cistercian monastery, Father Ludwik Leśny.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe entries from 1683, 1820, 1831, 1843, 1847, 1855, 1871, 1890, 1896, 1917, and 1927, compared with the scans. The history of the monastery according to Wikipedia and Father Edward Wojtusiak.
  • ProbableThe numbers of burials, baptisms, and marriages, counted automatically, as of November 2026. The monks’ surnames have been read in different ways (Jankowski and Dankowski, Klemp and Klemys), so the numbers may still change slightly.
  • To be checkedThe surnames of the prior Wincenty Kolor and of Father Eustachy.

What we don’t know

  • At whose request the monks came to the parish: the parish priest’s, the bishop’s, or the authorities’.
  • Whether the manor took the beggar woman from Kasina in, or whether she merely died there.
  • Where the monks lived while they worked in the parish.
