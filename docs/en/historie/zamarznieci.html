Dobra, Chyszówki, Wilczyce, Porąbka · 1845–1850 · a new story from the registers

Frozen to Death

In the winters of the hungry years, the priest several times entered the same cause of death: “ob nimium frigus,” from excessive cold. Those who died were most often people on the road.

Translated from the Polish original with the help of Claude. I give quotations from the registers in the original, with a translation.

On January 20, 1847, at the beginning of the year of hunger, 44-year-old Jakub, son of Antoni Pulka, a quarter-holder from house no. 2, was found dead in a field near Wilczyce. The priest briefly recorded why he died.

He was not buried until six days later. What he was doing in a field in winter, the register does not say. Ten people died in house no. 2 in 1847, most of them in the summer, of dysentery and hunger. That year is the subject of the story “The Year of Hunger”.

Four days in January 1848

A year later the famine was still going on, and people were exhausted after a year of hunger and typhus. Within four days in January 1848 the frost killed three people in the parish.

On January 23 in Chyszówki, thirteen-year-old Jan, son of Wojciech Drzyzga, a quarter-holder from house no. 33, was found. The priest wrote: “inventus frigore enectus,” found killed by the cold. He also appears in the story “The Great Snows”.

On January 25 in Dobra, in the house of Józef Kołodziej, no. 90, an unknown beggar boy of about fourteen died of the cold. The next day Antoni Wątor found a dead beggar woman on the road, about twenty-six years old. The priest did not know the name of either of them. For both he wrote the same thing.

Both were buried by the curate Wawrzyniec Rzeszódko, the same one who had buried Jan Drzyzga a few days earlier. In the same January the priest recorded in Dobra four more beggars from the parish who died of typhus.

Beggars and frost

In the parish registers for 1786–1926 there are nine deaths for which the priest explicitly entered frost, cold, or freezing. Eight of them fall in 1838–1850, and four in the two winters after the crop failure, 1847 and 1848. Four of the dead were beggars (as of November 2026).

  • March 31, 1845 · DobraKatarzyna Sekuła, a 58-year-old beggar woman, widow of the landless lodger Kazimierz, “ob nimium frigus mortua,” died of excessive cold.
  • January 27, 1850 · PorąbkaMarianna Hujka, a 54-year-old beggar woman, widow of Józef, “ob nimium frigus in via mortua,” died of the cold on the road.

The others, Alojzy Chochoł of 1838, Szymon Michalski of 1843, and Jan Judka, who died in 1907 of “zamrożenia” (freezing), are the subject of the story “The Great Snows”. There are more people in the registers who were found dead in winter in a field or by a road, but for them the priest entered hunger or destitution, or gave no cause.

The frost was the same for everyone, but it most often killed those who were on the road and those weakened by hunger. In 1847 the priest recorded the deaths of 63 beggars in the parish, and in 1848, 17. In no other year were there more than fourteen.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe quoted entries from 1845, 1847, 1848, and 1850, checked against the scans.
  • ProbableThe numbers of deaths from frost, of beggars who died, and of the dead in house no. 2 in Wilczyce, computed by machine from the transcriptions, as of November 2026. The priest may have recorded some such deaths without a cause. That frost killed people exhausted by hunger more easily.
  • To be checkedThe age of the unknown boy: the figures 14 are indistinct in the register.

What we don’t know

  • Who the unknown beggars who died in Dobra in January 1848 were, and where they came from.
  • Why Jakub Pulka went out into the fields in winter.
