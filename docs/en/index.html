    Nine out of ten Melchiors were born around the Feast of the Three Kings. Regina was once the most popular name for girls, but after 1900 the registers know only two.

    Births peaked in January and February and were fewest in July and August. Until the mid-19th century, nearly every child was baptized on the day of birth or the day after.

    A Calendar of Weddings

    More than a third of all weddings took place in February, while December saw only nine in 260 years. Almost no one married on a Friday.

    The Monks of Szczyrzyc

    A Cistercian was standing in for the parish priest as early as 1683. In the year of hunger and in the cholera summer, monks from Szczyrzyc buried the dead, and in 1871 the prior prepared the parish priest for death.

    The Organists of Dobra

    For more than two hundred years, the registers record the organists of Dobra. The one who played longest was Mateusz Rudnicki, organist and teacher, father of seventeen children, among them a priest.

    Aliases

    Wojciech Przybytek alias Wójt, Galas alias Fleszar, Węgrzyn recte Gąsior. People with two surnames in the parish registers.

    NewRead →     16th century – 1973Dobra and Jurków
    The Icon from Jurków

    In the altar of the old church in Dobra hangs a painting that, according to tradition, was brought by the water. It came from the Eastern-rite church of the Vlach shepherds at the foot of Mogielica.

    NewRead →     1694–1868Porąbka
    Porąbka, Another Lord’s Village

    In Dobra, consent to marry came from the Dobra manor; in Porąbka it came from the manors in Szczyrzyc and Janowice. There were few quarter-holders here, but there were farmers with full holdings.

    NewRead →     1697–1902Dobra, Wilczyce
    Inns and Innkeepers

    An innkeeper from Wilczyce complained to the lady of the manor because the parish priest had taken his oxen as payment for his mother’s funeral. Innkeepers in the parish registers, from Dobra peasants to newcomers from Moravia.

    NewRead →     1712–1923Dobra, Chyszówki, Półrzeczki, Jurków
    Water

    The flood of 1712 carried an icon away from Jurków, and in 1825 a flood took sixteen-year-old Anna from Chyszówki. Those who died in streams and wells were mostly children.

    NewRead →     1703–1910the whole parish
    Twins

    Agnieszka and Sebastian, Piotr and Paweł, Tomasz and Wiktoria: twins were given the names of saints whose feast days fell side by side. More than one in three died in their first year.

    NewRead →     1726–1732Dobra and the whole Dobra estate
    The Heaped Bushel

    The parish priest had a larger measure made for collecting his grain dues. Peasants from the whole estate took the case to the bishop’s court in Kraków and brought back a bushel measure of their own.

    NewRead →     1730–1922Dobra, Chyszówki
    The Roma in the Registers

    In 1730 a priest baptized the son of a Roma couple, accepting their marriage on the word of another Roma man. The godfather was the estate administrator.

    NewRead →     1734–1739Dobra, Jurków, Chyszówki, Zadziele
    Forty-One Charges

    The steward of the Dobra estate beat the serfs and did not pay them for their work. In 1739 the peasants and the lord of the manor sued him together. Among those he wronged was my ancestor from Jurków.

    NewRead →     1735Dobra and Jurków, Szerszenie
    The Brigand Chief’s Last Revel

    In the fall of 1735, the brigand chief Józef Baczyński was captured on the Dobra estate. In Jurków people say the inn stood at Szerszenie, a few dozen meters from today’s chapel.

    NewRead →     1774Jurków
    Married Again Twenty Years Later

    Tomasz and Agnieszka already had children when it came to light that they were related. The bishop ordered them to take their vows again.

    From a publicationRead →     1779–1899the whole parish
    A Hundred Years, Give or Take

    According to the registers, Zofia Stokłosionka of Porąbka lived to 115, Stanisław Stokłosa to 113, and Katarzyna Gdowicz to 110. Her marriage record says otherwise.

    NewRead →     1779–1913the whole parish
    What a House Number Tells

    A number in the register is not an address but the order in which the houses were built. In 1845 the whole parish was renumbered, and in Jurków two different houses had the number 123.

    NewRead →     1683–1917the whole parish
    The Millers

    The Stokłosa family were millers in Dobra, Jurków, and Gruszowiec. The miller Piotr Majchrzak signed his consent to his son’s marriage with a cross, and eighteen-month-old Agnieszka drowned in a millrace.

    NewRead →     1786–1924Gruszowiec
    Wolves in Gruszowiec

    In 1789 wolves tore apart thirteen-year-old Marianna, and five days earlier seven-year-old Paweł had died the same way in neighboring Porąbka. The newly read registers of Gruszowiec also tell of hunger, a tree, and a bridge.

    NewRead →     1799–1920the whole parish
    The Emperor’s Soldiers

    An uhlan of Archduke Charles’s regiment, a private of Regiment No. 57, a veteran with an invalid’s certificate, a man killed on the Nida River. Sons of the villages in the Austrian army and their traces in the registers.

    NewRead →     1807–1885the whole parish
    Smallpox and Vaccination

    Below the baptism of Maciej from Chyszówki, someone added in German “Impfung pro 842,” vaccination for the year 1842. In April the boy died of smallpox, unvaccinated.

    NewRead →     1818–1925Półrzeczki, Chyszówki, Jurków, Dobra
    People of the Forest

    Men crushed by trees they were felling, boys who fell from trees, and foresters from Bohemia in the forest lodges. The forest in the parish registers.

    NewRead →     1820–1900the whole parish
    A Quarter of a Farm

    “¼ Cm.” next to a father’s name meant a quarter of an old full-size peasant farm. In the mid-19th century, more than half of all fathers were recorded this way. Only Porąbka still had farmers with full holdings.

    NewRead →     1826–1920Jurków, Dobra
    One Bolt of Lightning

    In 1826 a single bolt of lightning killed a man and a child from two different houses. And in 1920 in Dobra, the priest recorded a death by lightning in February.

    From a publicationRead →     1826Jurków, Szyrszenie
    The Chapel at Szyrszenie

    Across from my ancestors’ house stands a chapel from 1826. Tradition says it was built by a Podgórni father when his son became a priest. I searched the registers for that priest.

    A Count at the Font

    A child of my ancestors in Jurków had Count and Countess Małachowski as godparents. It is the only such baptism in the whole parish.

    My familyRead →     1832–1907the whole parish
    Midwives without Schooling

    Agnieszka Chalcarz delivered 739 children, Anna Rapacz more than 300. For decades the register noted beside their names: “non examinata.”

    From a publicationRead →     1786–1899the whole parish
    Widows and Widowers

    More than one wedding in three involved a widower or a widow. A widower sometimes remarried a week after the funeral; a widow had to wait, as the law required.

    NewRead →     1838Dobra
    The Forester of Dobra

    My ancestor Józef Przybytek was the manor’s forester. For every log his neighbors cut, he was owed seven and a half kreuzers.

    My familyRead →     1840–1889four villages of the parish
    The First Year of Life

    One child in five never reached a first birthday. The first month was the most dangerous, and the priest most often wrote a single word: weakness.

    NewRead →     1842Półrzeczki, house no. 50
    Hungarian Tobacco

    Twenty-one-year-old Franciszek from Półrzeczki would not hand over his Hungarian tobacco to the soldiers guarding the border. He was killed by a bullet and buried in Krościenko.

    NewRead →     1829–1850Dobra, the manor
    The Manor Official of Dobra

    On March 1, 1846, during the Galician peasant revolt, an official of the Dobra manor was killed. The priest wrote only: on account of a crime. He was buried in Bochnia.

    NewRead →     1847the whole parish
    The Year of Hunger

    In Jurków 111 people died, in Chyszówki 118, and in Wilczyce nine times more than usual. Begging children from other places died in the fields.

    From a publicationRead →     1823–1925the whole parish
    With the Regiment’s Permission

    A soldier from Półrzeczki had the army’s permission, his bride-to-be the manor’s. Marriage permits came from Lviv, from Hungary, from the district office in Limanowa, and after the war from the Polish recruiting command.

    NewRead →     1845–1850Dobra, Chyszówki, Wilczyce, Porąbka
    Frozen to Death

    In January 1848, after the year of hunger, the frost killed three people in four days. Two of them were beggars whose names the priest did not know.

    NewRead →     1853Chyszówki, house no. 51
    Rakamaz, 1853

    A landless laborer from Chyszówki died in Hungary, on the Tisza. The news took four months to reach home.

    From a publicationRead →     1854/55Dobra, Chyszówki, Półrzeczki, Jurków
    The Great Snows

    People smothered by snow, and the sick whom the priest could not reach through the drifts. A winter in the middle of the hunger years.

    NewRead →     1854–1904Dobra, Jurków, Chyszówki
    Czechs and Moravians

    A road foreman from Červený Kostelec, a gardener from Bohemia, a gamekeeper from Moravia, and an itinerant gelder who died on the road in Jurków.

    NewRead →     1855Dobra, house no. 99
    The Doctor from Vienna

    A physician sent to fight cholera died in Dobra at the age of 34. A single entry in the register is all that remains of him.

    NewRead →     1855–1863the whole parish
    Father Józef Rapacz

    A new curate began burying the dead at the end of the cholera summer of 1855. A few weeks earlier, a peasant’s son with the same first and last name had died of cholera.

    NewRead →     1860–1893Dobra
    Neighbors of the Mosaic Faith

    The parish priest of Dobra also entered births in Jewish families in the baptism register, among them the children of the man who leased the liquor rights.

    NewRead →     1873–1920the whole parish
    To Ostrava and Overseas

    Notes in the margins of the registers lead to Hungary, to the mines of Ostrava, to Prussia, and to Chicago. And a shoemaker came to Dobra from Normandy.

    NewRead →     1873–1941Jurków, house no. 78
    A Lawmaker and a Mayor from Jurków

    Michał Łaskuda was mayor of Jurków and sat in three parliaments; his brother Szymon was the first mayor of the Dobra municipality. Szymon perished in Buchenwald in 1941.

    NewRead →     1873Zadziele and the whole parish
    1873 in Zadziele

    In the smallest village of the parish, fourteen people died in a single year. Smallpox took children, a mother died after giving birth, and in Dobra cholera killed three people in one house in three days.

    NewRead →     1786–1896the whole parish
    Death in Childbirth

    Nearly one in five married women who died before the age of forty-five died in childbirth or shortly after. The priest wrote briefly: propter partum.

    NewRead →     1883–1901Dobra, Porąbka, Półrzeczki
    The Railroad and the Steam Saw

    A stonemason from Belluno, an overseer from Tyrol, a builder from Bohemia, and architects as witnesses at a village wedding. That is how the railroad came to Dobra.

    NewRead →     1892–1953Jurków, house no. 108
    Father Leon of Jurków

    There was a priest in my family after all, only a hundred years after the chapel. Andrzej Podgórni of Jurków became a Dominican, and in 1944 he miraculously survived the attack on the monastery in Podkamień.

    My familyRead →     1892–1916the neighboring parish of Mszana Dolna
    To Pest and Chicago

    In the neighboring parish of Mszana Dolna, nineteen men died in Hungary over twenty years, and more than twenty children were born in Budapest. After 1907, Chicago appears in the registers.

    NewRead →     1892–1904the neighboring parish of Mszana Dolna
    Scarlet Fever, Dysentery, Measles

    In the summer of 1892, children began to die in Kasinka Mała. Over twelve years, scarlet fever, dysentery, measles, diphtheria, and whooping cough swept through the villages of Mszana Dolna parish.

    NewRead →     1894–1912the neighboring parish of Mszana Dolna
    Forest, Sawmill, and Water

    “He was killed in the forest,” “killed in the sawmill,” “killed by a cow,” “snake bite.” From 1897 on, the priests in Mszana Dolna recorded causes of death in Polish.

    NewRead →     1895–1965Dobra, house no. 202
    The Innkeeper’s Grandson

    In 1895 a boy was born in the innkeeper’s house in Dobra who went on to become a Czechoslovak legionnaire, an intelligence officer, and envoy to Italy. A note added to the register says where he died.

    NewRead →     1895–1906Mszana Górna, neighboring parish
    Great-Grandmother from Number 25

    A widow, four grown children, three weddings, and three funerals. Eleven years of one house in Mszana Górna, the house my great-grandmother came from.

    My familyRead →     1899–1909Mszana Dolna, neighboring parish
    The Count from Number 1

    Five children of Count Krasiński, of house number 1, were baptized in Mszana Dolna. The youngest was held at the font by its own brothers and sisters, and the countess sometimes stood godmother to her neighbors’ children.

    NewRead →     1901–1912Jurków, Chyszówki
    Sipka

    A dialect word the priest wrote next to children who died. In 1901 he himself explained what it meant.

    From a publicationRead →     1907Mszana Dolna, neighboring parish
    The Circus Director

    On August 20, 1907, a thirty-six-year-old Hungarian died of a wound in Mszana Dolna. The priest wrote his occupation in Polish, in parentheses: “dyrektor cyrku,” circus director.

    NewRead →     1913–1925Jurków
    A Parish Boundary through Jurków

    In 1913 Jurków got a priest of its own against the will of the parish priest of Dobra, who recorded burials in the death register as “without the parish priest’s consent.” The lower part of the village, Jurków Dolny, belongs to Dobra to this day.

    NewRead →     1914–1916the neighboring parish of Mszana Dolna
    The War in the Marriage Register

    In February 1914, 23 couples married. In February 1915, not a single one. When the young men went off to war, those who stood at the altar were mostly widowers.

    NewRead →     1916Chyszówki, Półrzeczki, Jurków
    A New Cemetery without the Parish Priest’s Consent

    The curate from Jurków buried the dead of Chyszówki in an unconsecrated cemetery. In the Dobra register, a note about it runs across the whole page.

    From a publicationRead →     1918six villages of the parish
    The Spanish Flu

    In the fall of 1918, influenza killed 18 people in Dobra, 15 in Półrzeczki, 10 in Chyszówki, and 9 in Porąbka. Those who died were mostly young adults.
