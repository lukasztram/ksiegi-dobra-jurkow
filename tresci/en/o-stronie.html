For more than two hundred years, the priests of Dobra, and later of Jurków, recorded every baptism, wedding, and funeral. These registers are the longest chronicle of our villages, though one written by hand, in Latin, and in columns.

From the author

I am a forester. In my daily work, what counts is the map, the measurement, and the source, not “it seems to me.” I apply the same standard to history. For years I have been collecting photographs of my family, the parish, and the municipality. In the fall of 2026 I began reading the old parish registers of Dobra, because that is where my mother’s family comes from. I was looking for my ancestors, and I found the stories of whole villages.

I read the registers together with Claude, an AI assistant. Claude deciphers the Latin handwriting entry by entry and does the counting. I choose the topics and contribute what I know about the area, its families, and its hamlets, and we check doubtful readings against the scans. This work has produced books for my family and two publications for local residents, on Jurków and on Chyszówki. Here I gather the most interesting stories from them and add new ones, straight from the registers.

A few of the stories concern my own family. Those I write in the first person.

How these stories are made

The Polish Genealogical Society (PTG) publishes scans of the registers online on its Metryki website. Since October 2026 they have been read according to a fixed set of instructions: dates, house numbers, first names, surnames, causes of death, and notes in the margins. The database now holds more than 70,000 entries: more than 54,000 from the registers of Dobra parish, nearly 16,000 from those of the neighboring parish of Mszana Dolna, and the rest from the registers of Niedźwiedź and Słopnice (as of November 2026). The Registers tab shows which registers have already been read.

Every story gives the register’s archival reference and the scan number, so anyone can look at the source for themselves. With every conclusion, I say how certain it is.

Principles

  • I write about people who died long ago. I do not write about the living.
  • The stories end in 1926, a hundred years before this site was created.
  • I describe suicides and children born out of wedlock without first names or surnames. Descendants of these families still live here today.
  • I do not copy the scans. I give the archival reference and a link to the Metryki PTG website.
  • I call a conjecture a conjecture.

How certain is this

  • CertainA reading checked against the scan, or a fact confirmed in several records.
  • ProbableOne reliable trace, with nothing to contradict it.
  • PossibleA conjecture that fits the facts but has no proof.
  • To be checkedA machine reading not yet checked against the scan.
