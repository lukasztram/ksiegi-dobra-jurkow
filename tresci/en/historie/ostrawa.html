A baptism register tells us more than when someone was born. From 1908, church law required the priest who performed a marriage to notify the parish of baptism. There the marriage was noted in the margin of the old entry. News of deaths abroad reached the registers in a similar way. These notes show where the children of our villages scattered.

To Hungary

The earliest traces lead to Hungary. In January 1873, 33-year-old Sebastian Smoleń of house no. 8 in Jurków died of cholera in Diósgyőr and was buried there. Diósgyőr is now part of Miskolc, a city of ironworks and steel. In August 1879 a fourteen-year-old boy from Dobra died in Hungary. In January 1892, 26-year-old Mikołaj Czyrnek of Jurków died in Budapest.

Whole families went to Budapest. In 1896 three children from parish families were born there, and the district office in Limanowa ordered them entered in the baptism register in Dobra. The mother of one of them, the wife of Józef Brudziana, had a Hungarian surname: Katarzyna Kovács. In 1898 a three-month-old child of Szymon Michałek, born in Pest, died in Jurków. In 1912 Antoni Kuchta of Jurków married in Budapest.

To Ostrava

Most of the notes, however, lead to Ostrava, a great center of coal and ironworks. Moravian Ostrava lay on the Moravian side of the Ostravice River, Polish Ostrava on the Silesian side. Today both are part of one city. The registers contain at least fourteen marriages contracted there in 1908–1920 and five deaths from 1891–1914.

In 1896 Stanisław Przybytek, recorded as a laborer in Moravian Ostrava, had his daughter Anna baptized in Dobra. The girl died a year later in Ostrava. So the family lived between work and home.

People started families in Ostrava. Jan Pałka of Dobra married Kunegunda Matacz in Polish Ostrava in 1908. In 1909 Józef Dusza of Dobra married Anna Havránek, who had a Czech surname, in Moravian Ostrava.

Siblings often left, one after another. From house no. 285 in Dobra, Anna Maludy married in Polish Ostrava in 1912, and her brother Józef married there in June 1915, in the middle of the war. From house no. 53 in Półrzeczki, Jan Kuchta married in Moravian Ostrava in 1908, and his younger brother Antoni in Polish Ostrava in 1920.

Not everyone came back. Jan Bolisęga of Dobra died in March 1914 in a hospital in Moravian Ostrava. He was 30.

To Prussia

Others went to Germany to earn money. In March 1912 Jan Kołodziej of house no. 106 in Jurków, a laborer, died in Prussia and was buried there. He was 30 and had married only a year earlier.

Across the ocean

Three notes lead to America. Szymon Brudziana of Dobra died in America on March 20, 1910, at the age of 18. Anna Dudzik of Półrzeczki married in Chicago on January 31, 1910, and the priest even noted her American address. Marianna Rzepka, also from Półrzeczki, married in America in 1915.

And someone who came from far away

People also traveled in the other direction. At the end of the 19th century, a shoemaker named Leon Lemperier, from the village of Ranchy near Bayeux in Normandy, lived in Dobra. With Anna Markowicz he had three daughters here, baptized in 1897–1901. For the first, Germana Jadwiga, the godparents were her grandparents in France, German and Armanda. The organist Kazimierz Mendalski and Maria Matacz stood at the font in their place. How a Frenchman ended up at the foot of Mogielica, the registers do not say.

These notes are only part of the story. The priest recorded a departure only when someone abroad married or died and the news reached the parish. How many people really left, and how many came back, the registers will not tell us.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe dates and places of the marriages and deaths in all the notes cited. The transcriptions were checked against the scans.
  • To be checkedThe surname of Anna Dudzik’s husband and the number of marriages in Ostrava. In several further notes the name of the city is read with uncertainty.

What we don’t know

  • How many people left the parish for work and how many came back.
  • Where they worked: in mines, in ironworks, on building sites?
  • How a shoemaker from Normandy came to Dobra.
