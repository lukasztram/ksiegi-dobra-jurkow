The forests below Mogielica, Łopień, and Ćwilin gave the villages timber for their cottages, firewood, and paid work. A large part of them belonged to the Dobra estate, and peasants worked in them felling trees and hauling timber. The death registers show what this work cost.

Killed by trees

The priest did not always describe the cause of death, but these accidents he recorded plainly:

  • Półrzeczki · 1820Mateusz Drzyzga, 50: “casu in silvis,” an accident in the forest.
  • Jurków · 1835Andrzej Michatek, 15: fell from a tree.
  • Chyszówki · 1836Maciej Czech, 61: a head injury while felling a tree in the forest.
  • Chyszówki · 1838Piotr Czopek, 18: fell from a tree.
  • Półrzeczki · 1845Wojciech Pajdzik, 31, a landless lodger: crushed by a tree, died on the spot.
  • Dobra · 1889Stanisław Stokłosa, 15: crushed by a cart loaded with branches.
  • Półrzeczki · 1894Jan Kaim, 40, a farmer: crushed by a tree in the forest.
  • Chyszówki · 1903Michał Zawada, 26: “w lesie zabity,” killed in the forest.
  • Dobra · 1914Marcin Pałka, 50: “w lesie zabity,” killed in the forest.
  • Chyszówki · 1925Maciej Żak, 57: “stłuczenie drzewem,” struck by a tree.

In those years several other men were crushed by a cart or a wheel, but the priest did not record what they were hauling at the time.

Keepers of the forest

The forests were watched over by guards, and later by foresters. In 1818–1821 Marcin Staliński, recorded as “custos silvarum,” forest guard, held children at baptism in Jurków and Dobra. In 1838 the manor’s forester was Józef Przybytek, who is the subject of the story {{tytul:lesniczy}}.

From the 1870s on, trained foresters appear in the registers, often with foreign surnames. In December 1871 a daughter of Antoni Hüttel, forester on the estate of Edward Zwierzyna, was baptized in Dobra. Both parents came from Bohemia. The godfather was another forester, Jan Göttmann.

The Göttmanns were foresters from father to son. In 1891 the forester was Jan Göttmann, son of the forester Jan. In 1891–1899 Edward Kopetschny, son of Jerzy, the administrator of the Dobra estate, was also a forester.

Foresters’ lodges

For years two houses in the parish were occupied by a succession of men of the forest. In Jurków it was house no. 105. In 1881 the forest ranger Edward Höpfl lived there. In 1885 the 24-year-old forest ranger Jan Krusch, a native of Moravia, died there of pneumonia. In 1891 a son of the forester Edward Kopetschny was born there, and in 1898–1906 the forester Antoni Steiner lived there; he died in 1906, aged 42.

In Półrzeczki it was house no. 104. In 1888 the forest guard Franciszek Kamiński lived there; his father-in-law was also a forest guard. In 1895 the forester here was Alfred Brauner, and in 1899–1901 Antoni Douša. The Doušas lost two little sons here, in 1899 and in 1901.

With the railroad came sawmills. In 1887–1890 the registers of Dobra mention sawyers working for the railroad, and in Półrzeczki the engineer of a steam saw, as told in the story {{tytul:kolej}}.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe forest accidents and forestry occupations recorded in the registers. The transcriptions were checked against the scans.
  • ProbableThat house no. 105 in Jurków and house no. 104 in Półrzeczki were foresters’ lodges. We infer this from the fact that one forester or forest ranger after another lived in them.
  • To be checkedThe spelling of foreign surnames, for example Höpfl, Krusch, and Douša, and the surname of the estate owner in 1871, written “Zwierzyna.”

What we don’t know

  • How many accidents during work in the forest the priest recorded without describing the cause.
  • Who owned the forests in particular years. The owners of the Dobra estate changed several times in the 19th century.
