In the 19th century, next to a child’s father the priest wrote down not only his first name and surname but also his standing in the village. Most often this was the abbreviation “Cm.” with a fraction: “½ Cm.,” “¼ Cm.,” “⅛ Cm.” “Cmeto” is Latin for “kmieć,” a farmer with a full holding, that is, a whole farm of the old kind. The fraction showed what part of such a holding the father had.

Alongside the farmers with holdings were the cottagers, “hortulani,” who had a cottage and a garden but little farmland. Lowest of all were the lodgers, “inquilini.” They had no cottage of their own; they lived in a corner of a farmer’s house and worked for others.

A village of quarter-holders

In 1820–1879, more than half of the fathers of baptized children were recorded as quarter-holders, and in Dobra, Jurków, Chyszówki, and Półrzeczki three-fifths. One in eight had half a holding, one in eleven an eighth, and one in ten was a lodger. The old whole farms had already been divided into quarters and eighths (as of November 2026).

Porąbka was different

Over those sixty years the priest recorded a farmer with a full holding 63 times, but almost exclusively in Porąbka. There were few quarter-holders there. Families from Porąbka were mostly cottagers and lodgers, and alongside them half-holders and farmers with a whole holding, for whom the priest wrote “1 Cm.” or “Cmetonis.” At that time Porąbka belonged to a different manor than Dobra: its inhabitants received permission to marry from the Janowice estate (“dominium”). This may have been the reason for the different division of land, but the registers do not explain it. The story {{tytul:porabka}} tells about it.

In the 18th century the villages still had “zarębnicy,” who farmed “zarębki,” that is, fields cleared out of the forest. There were also “noworolnicy” (“new holders”), settled by the lady of the manor on manor land. Both groups appear in the disputes with the parish priest and with the steward described in the stories {{tytul:korzec}} and {{tytul:rzadca}}. Whether the name of the Noworolniki settlement in Dobra comes from the noworolnicy is not known.

The end of the fractions

In the 1880s the fractions begin to disappear from the registers. The priest started writing “agricola,” farmer, and after 1900 he recorded almost every father as “hortulanus,” cottager. This was a change of formula in the registers, not in the village. After emancipation the peasants owned their land, and the old division into holdings lost its meaning.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe fractions and the social standing recorded next to fathers. The quoted entry has been compared with the scan.
  • ProbableThe numbers and percentages, counted automatically from transcriptions of the registers of all nine villages, as of November 2026. In some entries the fraction refers to the child’s grandfather rather than the father, so the numbers describe families rather than the fathers themselves. Also that after 1880 only the formula changed, not the village.
  • PossibleA link between the name of the Noworolniki settlement and the noworolnicy of old. Also that the different division of land in Porąbka was connected with its different manor.

What we don’t know

  • How many morgs (a morg was about 1.4 acres) a full holding had in our villages, and how many a quarter of one. Only the land registers and cadastral maps give that.
