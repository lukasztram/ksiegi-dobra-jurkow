The tavern belonged to the manor. The lord held the right of propination, the monopoly on alcohol: only he could brew beer, distill vodka, and sell drink. He leased out the taverns and the brewery; such a lease was called arenda. In the registers a tavern keeper appears as “caupo” or “tabernator,” a leaseholder as “arendator” or “propinator,” and a brewer as “braxator.”

The tavern keeper of Wilczyce and the parish priest’s oxen

The oldest stories about tavern keepers survive in court records from the 1730s. At that time the estate had a tavern keeper named Szymon Wrona, to whom the manor’s steward issued salt in a leaky barrel. The brewery in Dobra was held by Jewish leaseholders. In Wilczyce the tavern was kept by a man named Węgrzyn. It was he, as one of the witnesses testified in 1731, who first complained to the lady of the manor about the parish priest and his oversized measure for the “meszne” (the grain due paid to the parish priest). He had his reason: when his mother died, the priest took oxen from him for the funeral. The lady of the manor reportedly scolded him: “Traitor, why don’t you report to me what goes on in the village?” The whole dispute is described in the story {{tytul:korzec}}.

In 1735, according to most accounts in a tavern on the Dobra estate, the outlaw chief Józef Baczyński was captured. This is told in the story {{tytul:harnas}}.

Tavern keepers as godparents

In 1758 the register mentions Antoni Pawlak, the tavern keeper of Dobra. Toward the end of the 18th century there are several tavern keepers in Dobra at once. In 1788–1797 Franciszek Nowomiejski, Antoni Ranos, Szymon Deński, Wawrzyniec Hadkański, and Wojciech Judka appear in the baptism register as godparents, all recorded as “caupo.” They were peasants, sometimes with the note “caupo rusticus.” People were glad to ask them to be godparents, and did so often, sometimes together with their wives.

In 1795 Wojciech Judka was the “propinator in Dobra.” He died in the spring of the following year.

In the same years the brewer Józef Bernkop lived in Dobra. The brewers of old also left surnames behind: in 18th-century Porąbka there lived families named Piwowarek and Piwowar, and in Dobra the Piwowarczyk family (“piwowar” is Polish for brewer).

House no. 202

In the second half of the 19th century the manor’s tavern in Dobra was run by leaseholders from far away. At that time it was most likely in house no. 202. In 1873–1876 Józef Menk lived there, a propinator of the Mosaic faith, whose daughters are the subject of the story {{tytul:sasiedzi}}. In 1884 the propinator was Alojzy Morawek.

In the 1890s the owner of the tavern, “possessor cauponae,” was Franciszek Wesely. His daughter Zofia married Włodzimierz Vaněk from Moravia, the accountant of the Dobra estate. Their son, born in house no. 202 in 1895, became a Czechoslovak legionnaire, an intelligence officer, and a diplomat. He is the subject of the story {{tytul:vanek}}.

In Jurków the registers record no tavern at all. Nor is there one on the cadastral map of 1845. Local memory places one in Szerszenie, but that goes back to the time of the outlaw chief.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe tavern keepers, propinators, and the brewer recorded in the registers. The transcriptions were checked against the scans.
  • ProbableThe stories from the 1730s. We know them from an article by Edward Wojtusiak, who described the court records.
  • To be checkedThe spelling of some surnames, for example Deński and Hadkański. The priest wrote them a little differently each time.

What we don’t know

  • How many taverns there were in the parish and where they stood. We know the house number only for the tavern keeper Judka, no. 101 in 1796, and for the leaseholders of 1873–1902, no. 202.
  • Who kept the taverns in Gruszowiec, Porąbka, Chyszówki, and Półrzeczki.
