Most parts of Jurków are named after surnames: Siciarze, Czerniki, Szerszenie, Krawce, Rubisie, Urbasie, Wróblówka, Trzopki, Pólkówka, Do Maludego, Gdowicze, Kuligi, Zawady, Judy, Parużniki. The same surnames have stood in the parish registers since the 17th century. A hamlet is simply the place where one family lived for generations.

A single baptism record from February 1680 already brings three such families together. Maciej, son of Łukasz Szerszeń, was baptized, and the godparents were Wojciech Czernek and Katarzyna, daughter of Sabur.

Mentions are all people with the given surname, in every role, in the Jurków records: parents, godparents, witnesses, and the deceased. “First record” is the first appearance in the surviving registers, not the beginning of the family.

Some families disappeared, but the names remained. The Siciarz family does not appear in the Jurków records after 1774, nor the Wróbel family after 1736, yet their hamlets survive to this day. The Maludy and Krawiec families were among the largest in the 18th century; by the end of the 19th century they were baptizing only the occasional child, but the names Do Maludego and Krawce remained.

In Trzopki stands house no. 2, where my great-grandmother Kunegunda came from. The Trzopek family, in the registers also Czopek, lived in it from the 18th century on.

In the old registers the same surname takes many forms. The priest wrote a family name in the genitive plural, for example “Kaczorów” (of the Kaczors), named a daughter after her father “Maludzionka” or, as in the 1680 record, “Saburonka,” and a wife “Rapaczowa.” The spelling changed too: Czernek turned into Cyrnek, and then into Czyrnek.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe family surnames in the registers since the 17th century and their connection with the names of the hamlets.
  • To be checkedThe dates of the first records. The baptism registers are silent for 1704–1719, and the oldest records often do not give the village.

What we don’t know

  • Where the name of Jurków itself came from. In a document of 1364 the village is called “Jurze.”
  • Where exactly the oldest houses stood of the families that gave the hamlets their names.
