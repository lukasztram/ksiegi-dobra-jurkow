The priest recorded the first death from the Spanish flu on October 11, 1918, in two villages at once. In Chyszówki a 35-year-old married woman died, and in Półrzeczki fifteen-year-old Jan Kołodziej. In the following days the disease went from house to house.

The worst day was October 14 in Półrzeczki. In house no. 37, 24-year-old Kunegunda Ogryzek died, two days after giving birth to a son. The same day her newborn son Józef died, and also 22-year-old Andrzej and 19-year-old Anna Ogryzek from the same house. That day the Spanish flu took three more people in Półrzeczki; the oldest was the 90-year-old widow Maria Rapacz. The four funerals from one cottage were held together, on October 16.

In Chyszówki two men of the Bolisęga family died in house no. 24: 28-year-old Jan on October 19 and 20-year-old Franciszek three days later. In house no. 48, nine-month-old Jan Sroka died on October 30, and his 30-year-old mother Maria the next day.

In Dobra the disease appeared a few days later and lasted longer, until early December. In house no. 7, 40-year-old Helena Wątor died, and two days after her, her four-year-old son Jan. In house no. 12, the Spanish flu took two sisters in November, five-year-old Wiktoria and eleven-year-old Marianna.

From the duplicate registers, transcribed in November 2026, we know that the Spanish flu also reached Porąbka, Łostówka, and Gruszowiec. In Porąbka the first to die of it, on October 15, was five-year-old Stanisław Cygal of house no. 30. By the end of the year the priest had entered the Spanish flu there for nine of the dead, among them the Woźniak siblings: eighteen-year-old Stanisław, who died on November 9, and 22-year-old Wiktoria, who died two weeks after him. In Łostówka, twenty-year-old Szymon Kołodziej and 61-year-old Józef Michałek died on October 21. Next to the first of them the priest wrote in Polish: “grypa hiszpańska” (Spanish influenza).

Altogether, in the six villages whose registers for these months have been transcribed, the priest recorded the Spanish flu for 55 of the dead: 18 in Dobra, 15 in Półrzeczki, 10 in Chyszówki, 9 in Porąbka, 2 in Łostówka, and 1 in Gruszowiec (as of November 2026). What happened in Jurków is not known, because the death registers of Jurków for 1913–1918 have not been preserved on the Metryki website of the Polish Genealogical Society (PTG).

The Spanish flu killed differently from ordinary diseases. In ordinary years almost half of those buried were children under 15. In the fall of 1918 there were only fourteen children among those who died of the Spanish flu. More than two-fifths, 23 people out of 55, were between 15 and 40. People in the prime of life were dying: mothers of small children and young people before marriage.

In those same weeks the war was ending and Poland was regaining its independence. The death registers from that fall show only one funeral after another.

In Porąbka the priest was still entering the Spanish flu in 1919, for two of the dead. A second, weaker wave came at the beginning of 1920. Four people died of the Spanish flu in Chyszówki then, four in Półrzeczki, and one in Dobra.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe dates, names, ages, and causes of death of the people mentioned, including those from Porąbka and Łostówka. The transcriptions were checked against the scans.
  • To be checkedThe death counts (as of November 2026) come from the transcription of all the entries from that fall. Not every one of them was checked against the scan.

What we don’t know

  • How many people died of the Spanish flu in Jurków, Wilczyce, and Zadziele. From Gruszowiec we know of only one such death.
  • How many people fell ill and recovered. The registers record only deaths.
