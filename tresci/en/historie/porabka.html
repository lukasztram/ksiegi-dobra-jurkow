Porąbka has belonged to the parish of Dobra for centuries. Its people were baptized, married, and buried by the priest from Dobra, and the entries from Porąbka stand in the same registers as those from Dobra, Jurków, or Gruszowiec. Yet the registers show that Porąbka had a different lord from the rest of the parish.

Consent from another manor

In the first half of the 19th century a peasant needed the consent of the manor to marry, that is, of the dominium, which in Galicia (the Austrian part of partitioned Poland) was also a government office. In the 1830s and 1840s the priest noted at each wedding which dominium had issued the consent. For weddings from Dobra, Gruszowiec, Wilczyce, Zadziele, and Łostówka it was the dominium of Dobra. For weddings from Porąbka it was different. In 1833 and 1834 consent was given by the manor in Szczyrzyc, and after that, until 1853, by the dominium of Janowice. There are more than forty such notes in the Porąbka marriage register (as of November 2026). The dominium of Dobra appears in it only a few times, usually next to a consent from Janowice, when one of the couple came from the Dobra estate. The priest then recorded two consents, from two manors.

The change can be seen on the same page of the register. The wedding of 1834 still has the consent “a Dnio Scyrzyc,” and the first wedding of 1835 already has “a Dnio Janowice.”

Janowice lies just below Szczyrzyc. The Cistercian abbey there owned villages and manor farms in the area, and according to Father Edward Wojtusiak, in the 18th century its estates reached as far as Porąbka, where the monks had a manor farm with a brewery. The monks from Szczyrzyc in the parish are the subject of the story {{tytul:cystersi}}.

In 1794 the Austrian authorities dissolved the abbey. The monks did not return to Szczyrzyc until 1834, and the monastery’s former estates by then belonged to other owners. In 1868 the Cistercians bought part of them back from the Dzieduszycki family. So the consents from Janowice were most likely issued by the administration of the former monastic estates, to which Porąbka belonged.

Few quarters, many smallholders

A different lord could also mean differently divided farms. In Dobra and Gruszowiec the priest at a baptism most often recorded the child’s father or grandfather as a quarter-holder, “¼ Cm.,” as told in the story {{tytul:cwierc-roli}}. In Porąbka there were almost no quarter holdings.

Porąbka was home above all to smallholders, who had a cottage and a garden but little field land, and to landless lodgers, who did not even have a cottage of their own. Alongside them lived families of half-holders and of kmieć farmers, for whom the priest wrote no fraction at all, or wrote plainly “1 Cm.,” one kmieć, that is, a full holding.

The farm steward ran the manor’s own farm, the folwark. Why the land in Porąbka was divided differently from Dobra, the registers do not say. Perhaps a different lord divided the farms differently, or perhaps the manor farm needed many hands, and it was precisely the smallholders and landless lodgers who supplied them.

House no. 1

People of a different estate also lived in Porąbka. In January 1694 the godmother of two children from Porąbka was “Generosa Domina Sophia Czyglicowa de Porąbka,” the noblewoman Zofia Czyglicowa of Porąbka.

On November 24, 1779, the 65-year-old “Dominus Antonius Majeronowski,” Mr. Antoni Majeronowski, died in house no. 1. He was buried in the church, not in the cemetery like the peasants. In 1837 the same house no. 1 was home to Jan Urban, recorded at his son’s baptism as “Posessor de Porąbka,” that is, the leaseholder of Porąbka. His son’s godparents were Mateusz Rudnicki, organist and teacher in Dobra, who is the subject of the story {{tytul:organisci}}, and Miss Elżbieta Moliteris.

So house no. 1 was probably the manor house. It was lived in by leaseholders and administrators, not by the owner, because the owner, first the monastery and then the successive lords of the former monastic estates, was elsewhere.

What we don’t know

  • Since when Porąbka belonged to the Cistercians, and who owned it between 1794 and 1868.
  • How large a full holding was in Porąbka and whether it was larger than in Dobra.
  • Who Zofia Czyglicowa was, and whether the Majeronowskis were leaseholders of the monastery.
