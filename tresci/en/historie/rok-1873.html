Zadziele was the smallest village in the parish. In the 1870s it had just over twenty houses, and in an ordinary year the priest entered four or five burials in its death register. In 1873 he entered fourteen. Zadziele had not had so many deaths in the famine year of 1847, nor in the cholera summer of 1855, nor in any other year known from the registers (as of November 2026).

Smallpox in spring

In the spring of 1873 smallpox spread through the whole parish. In May alone it killed 22 people in the nine villages, almost all of them children. It reached Zadziele at the end of May. In house no. 16 Franciszek Stokłosa and Marianna, née Jarosz, lost two sons: eight-year-old Wojciech died on May 26, and nine-month-old Szymon on June 6. On June 20 smallpox took two-week-old Marianna Ranosz of house no. 3.

In the whole parish smallpox killed 48 people in 1873, 37 of them children under five. Smallpox and vaccination are the subject of the story {{tytul:ospa}}.

September

On September 11, in house no. 21, Katarzyna was born, daughter of Jan Trzópek, a quarter-holding farmer, and Katarzyna, née Miśkowiec. The mother was 26. She died on September 20, nine days after giving birth. The priest wrote: “propter partum,” because of childbirth. Two days after her mother, the eleven-day-old baby girl died. How often childbirth ended in death is the subject of the story {{tytul:porod}}.

Then came dysentery. On October 6 it killed eighteen-month-old Józef Drąg of house no. 22. In autumn and winter the older people died. In house no. 20, where week-old Antoni, son of Stanisław Ogryzek, had died in April, the 65-year-old landless lodger Franciszka Smoleń died in November, and on Christmas Eve the 47-year-old widow Teresa Ogryzek died of consumption. The next year only one person died in Zadziele.

A year of three diseases

Zadziele was not an exception. In 1873 many more people than usual died in almost the whole parish.

An “ordinary year” is the average for 1850–1889, leaving out the cholera year 1855 and 1873 itself. In Wilczyce four times as many people died in 1873 as usual, most of them of smallpox. In Półrzeczki dysentery raged in August and September: the priest entered it for thirteen of the dead.

Cholera came to Dobra in the summer. The first to die of it, on July 26, was 44-year-old Maciej Mycoń of house no. 147. Two days later, on the same day, his father Jan and his father’s wife Gertruda died. The priest recorded Jan as already the widower of Gertruda.

The next day the 20-year-old soldier Jan Gąsior of house no. 13 died of cholera. By August 23 cholera had taken nine people in Dobra. For one of the dead the priest noted that he had not received the sacraments “propter negligentiam domesticorum,” through the negligence of his household.

Cholera also reached those who were far from home. In January 1873 Sebastian Smoleń of Jurków died of it in Diósgyőr in Hungary, as told in the story {{tytul:ostrawa}}. In October the priest entered in the Chyszówki register 32-year-old Józef Rapacz, son of the landless lodger Wawrzyniec: “in Bierzanow mortuus et ibidem sepultus,” died in Bieżanów and buried there. Cause: cholera. A curate who bore the same first name and surname is the subject of the story {{tytul:ksiadz-rapacz}}.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe 1873 entries cited from Zadziele, Dobra, and Chyszówki, checked against the scans.
  • ProbableThe death counts and the averages for an ordinary year, counted by machine from the transcriptions of the main registers, as of November 2026. The number of houses in Zadziele, estimated from the highest house numbers in the registers. That “Bierzanow” is Bieżanów near Kraków.

What we don’t know

  • Where the cholera came to Dobra from and why it began in house no. 147.
  • Whether the children who died of smallpox in Zadziele had been vaccinated.
  • What Józef Rapacz of Chyszówki was doing in Bieżanów.
