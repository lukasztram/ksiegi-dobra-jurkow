On December 14, 1831, in the church in Dobra, a boy named Józef was baptized, the son of Józef Podgórny and Agnieszka, née Trzopek, of house no. 5 in Jurków. They are my ancestors on my mother’s side, an ordinary peasant family. Yet in the godparents’ column the priest wrote two unusual names.

The baptism was performed by Father Ferdynand Wilczek, then the parish priest of Kasina. Luck was not on the child’s side. Next to the entry there is a small cross, and the death register records Józef’s death on Christmas Day 1831. He lived thirteen days. His father died eight months later, on August 11, 1832.

Count Ludwik Jakub Małachowski (1785–1856) inherited the Dobra estate from his uncle, Stanisław Małachowski, Marshal of the Four-Year Sejm (the Polish parliament of 1788–1792). He lived in Warsaw and in Białaczów, was a senator of the Kingdom of Poland, and on January 25, 1831, signed the act dethroning Tsar Nicholas I. According to a regional historian, he was in Dobra only once: in that very year, 1831, for three months. After the fall of the November Uprising against Russia, he had reason to wait things out on his estate in Galicia (the Austrian part of partitioned Poland), beyond Russia’s reach.

Did the count stand godfather to other children as well? In 1,373 Jurków baptisms from 1786–1840, and in 212 baptisms from seven villages of the parish between January 1831 and March 1832, the count and countess appear only once, at house no. 5.

Why there, of all places? This family had been close to the manor even before. In 1826 their son Jan was held at the font by the estate administrator, acting on behalf of the lord of Dobra, and by the wife of the estate’s leaseholder, and in 1829 another of their children was again held by the leaseholder’s wife. In that same year, 1826, a masonry chapel of the Holy Family went up opposite house no. 5. According to local tradition, it was built by a father from the Podgórni family in thanksgiving for his son becoming a priest. The chapel still stands, and in May 2026 the parish celebrated its bicentennial.

The manor remembered the family even after the father’s death. When in 1838 his daughter Marianna, sixteen according to the record, married the manor’s forester Józef Przybytek, consent to the marriage was given by “the guardians and the Lord and Lady,” that is, the manor.

I descend from Józef and Agnieszka twice over. One line runs through their daughter Marianna and the forester, the other through Jan, the son baptized in 1826 with the manor as godparents. Jan of house no. 5 is my great-great-great-grandfather. Their descendants, my great-grandfather Józef and my great-grandmother Kunegunda, were therefore related, and in 1915 they needed a bishop’s dispensation to marry.

About twenty years ago I stopped by the old house no. 5. The elderly lady who lived there asked me at length where I was from and finally said: “We’re family.” The house is gone now. The registers showed that she was right.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe godparents and the dates of baptism and death. Reading compared with the scans.
  • ProbableThe count’s stay in Dobra in 1831. A regional historian reports it; I have not seen the document.
  • PossibleA connection between the godparent ties and the chapel and the priest in the family.

What we don’t know

  • Who the priest from the Podgórni family was, for whose vocation the chapel was built. The records of the 1827 parish visitation in the Diocesan Archive in Tarnów may tell the most.
  • Whether the manor stood godparent to children in other villages of the Dobra estate.
