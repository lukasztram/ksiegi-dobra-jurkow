In the summer of 1855 cholera was decimating the parish of Dobra. The dead were buried by the parish priest, Jakub Jana, helped by a Cistercian from Szczyrzyc, Father Maksymilian Klemp, as told in the story {{tytul:cystersi}}. In September a new signature appears in the registers: “Josephus Rapacz Cooperator,” Józef Rapacz, cooperator, that is, curate.

Husband and wife

On September 8, 1855, 57-year-old Jan Walczak, a quarter-holder (a farmer with a quarter-size holding) from house no. 27 in Półrzeczki, died of cholera. The parish priest buried him. Four days later his widow, 53-year-old Agnieszka, née Fudalej, died of cholera. She was buried by the new curate. It is one of his first entries in the parish registers.

The cholera was already dying out. In the fall of 1855 Father Rapacz entered it at burials a few more times, the last time on October 22 in Półrzeczki.

Two and a half years

Józef Rapacz was the curate in Dobra until April 1858. He left more than five hundred entries in the parish registers: he baptized about 280 children, buried about 180 of the dead, and blessed more than 70 marriages (as of November 2026). He worked in all nine villages of the parish, most often in Dobra, Półrzeczki, and Jurków.

In Chyszówki the midwife at many of the births of children he baptized was Tekla Rapacz. Whether she was his relative, the registers do not say.

Namesakes

The surname Rapacz was very common in the parish. In 1786–1900 roughly one baptized child in seventy bore it, and in that period twenty boys named Józef Rapacz alone were baptized.

One of them died just before the new curate arrived. On July 21, 1855, 26-year-old Józef, son of Kazimierz Rapacz, died of cholera in Półrzeczki. He was buried by Father Maksymilian Klemp.

He was a peasant’s son, not a priest. A few weeks later a priest with the same first name and surname was burying the parish’s dead from the same disease. Yet another Józef Rapacz, the 32-year-old son of a landless lodger from Chyszówki, died of cholera in 1873 in Bieżanów, as told in the story {{tytul:rok-1873}}.

Where the curate came from is not known. He may have been a son of one of the Rapacz families of the parish, but the registers do not confirm this.

Parish priest in Mszana

In March 1863 Józef Rapacz appears in the parish registers once more. On March 10 in Wilczyce, in house no. 28, he baptized Wojciech, son of Jan Dudzik and Regina, née Wątor. His signature was no longer that of a curate.

So five years after leaving Dobra he was the parish priest in Mszana, probably Mszana Dolna, the neighboring parish to which part of Łostówka belonged. Why he baptized a child from Wilczyce, the register does not say.

How certain is this

  • CertainThat Józef Rapacz was the cooperator in Dobra in 1855–1858: his signature stands under hundreds of entries. The quoted entries from 1855 and 1863, checked against the scans.
  • ProbableThe counts of his entries and the number of Rapacz children baptized, computed by machine from the transcriptions, as of November 2026. That he was the parish priest in Mszana Dolna: the end of the word “Parochus” is covered in the register, and the name “Mszana” alone does not say whether Mszana Dolna is meant.
  • To be checkedWhere he came from, how old he was, and when he died. The answer may be found in the printed directories of the clergy, known as schematisms, of the dioceses of Tarnów and Kraków from the 1850s and 1860s.

What we don’t know

  • Whether the midwife Tekla Rapacz of Chyszówki was his relative.
  • How long he was the parish priest in Mszana and where he went afterward.
