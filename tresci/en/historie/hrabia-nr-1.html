In the registers of the neighboring parish of Mszana Dolna, almost every father of a baptized child is an “agricola,” a farmer, and his house has a two- or three-digit number. Number 1 is different. That is where the manor stood. In January 1899 the estates of Mszana Dolna, Glisne, and Słomka, previously owned by Władysław Szujski, passed to Maria, née Łęska, and her husband, Count Henryk Krasiński.

Five baptisms

On November 27, 1899, the parish priest, Father Wincenty Jankowski, baptized a girl born four days earlier. The entry is longer than the others, because each of the parents has three names, and the priest carefully noted this with the abbreviation “trinom.” (of three names).

In the last column the priest added three dates: “c. 5/10 897; p. 29/8 866; m. 27/11 877.” He did the same for peasant children: the first is the parents’ wedding day, the next two are the birth dates of the father and the mother. Here it can be checked, because the Krasińskis have been described by genealogists. The wedding took place on October 5, 1897, in Vilnius, exactly as the priest recorded. The countess was born on November 27. The count was born on the 29th of the month, though according to published accounts in April, not August. He was 33. So the daughter was baptized on her mother’s birthday.

At the first three baptisms the godparents were relatives from both families. The last two are different. In 1905 the godfather was Count Hubert Antoni Krasiński. That was the name of the child’s grandfather, but he had died in 1890. It was also the name of the count and countess’s eldest son, who was then not quite seven. In 1909 there is no longer any doubt: the youngest son was held at the font by his own brother and sister, seven-year-old Henryk and ten-year-old Maria. The priest recorded them with their titles, “comes” and “comitissa,” and next to the sister he added “virgo,” maiden.

The boy baptized in 1905 later became a Benedictine and is known as Father Cyryl Krasiński. The eldest of the siblings, Hubert, does not appear in the Mszana copies, although according to one source he was born here in November 1898.

The 1905 baptism looks different in the register from the others. It has no number and does not stand in line with the rest. Someone squeezed it in small handwriting into the printed heading of the page, above the first row. It looks as if it was added later, when there was no free space left on the page.

Since when in Mszana

Some accounts say that Henryk Krasiński settled in Mszana Dolna only after World War I, when he lost his estates in Ukraine. The baptism register says otherwise. From November 1899 on, his children were born at number 1 and were baptized by the local parish priest. A list of estate owners from 1902 names Dr. Henryk and Maria Krasiński in Mszana Dolna, Glisne, and Słomka.

It was they who built the manor house that still stands in the park in Mszana. According to one account, it was finished in 1905. The count died in Mszana Dolna on September 20, 1928.

Midwives

For peasant children from Mszana Dolna in these years, the midwife column almost always reads Wiktoria Średniawska. Other midwives came to the manor. The first two daughters were delivered by Julia Bojanowska. Only for the son in 1902 was Średniawska recorded. For the last birth, in December 1909, a midwife was brought in from the city: “Helena Bachowska e Cracovia,” from Kraków. Village midwives are the subject of the story {{tytul:polozne}}.

A countess at a peasant font

The count was not the only one living at number 1. In 1890–1916, fifty baptisms, marriages, and funerals were recorded there. These are the families of people who worked at the manor or rented lodgings in its buildings: an assistant official, a per-diem clerk, a court clerk, a post office clerk. Many of the names do not sound like highlander names: Dubový, Kunzig, Reichl, Schaffer. In 1904 a daughter of the Protestant Jerzy Kunz and Elżbieta, née Bischoff, was baptized there.

The Krasińskis sometimes stood as godparents to the children of their neighbors at number 1. On September 11, 1904, the countess held Maria Wiktoria, daughter of Józef Smoła, at the font. The other godparent was Franciszek Łabuz, and the priest recorded the countess as “Maria Krasińska ux. Henrici Comitissa,” wife of Henryk, countess.

In April 1906 and in November 1908 the count and countess together were godparents to children of Ignacy Żarowski, who also lived at number 1. The first time, the priest, while writing the rest of the entry in Latin, entered the godparents in Polish: “Piotr Zygmunt Henryk hr. Krasiński, Maryja hr. Krasińska.”

In the parish of Dobra, too, the lords of the manor stood at the font alongside peasants. This is told in the stories {{tytul:hrabia-przy-chrzcielnicy}} and {{tytul:mandatariusz}}.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe five baptisms of the Krasiński children, with dates, names, and godparents, and the baptisms of 1904 and 1906 at which the Krasińskis were godparents. I checked every entry against the scan.
  • ProbableThat the dates after the abbreviations “p.” and “m.” are the birth dates of the child’s father and mother: for the Krasińskis, the day matches the published accounts in both cases. The number of entries under number 1, counted from the transcriptions. That the godfather in 1905 was the child’s six-year-old brother. The circumstances of the takeover of the estates in 1899: sources differ on whether they were bought by the countess or by her father, Hilary Łęski.
  • PossibleThat the other residents of number 1 worked at the manor. The register gives their occupations but does not say whom they served.
  • To be checkedThe count’s month of birth (register: August; published accounts: April) and the countess’s year of birth (register: 1877; published accounts: 1874). The surname Żarowski (perhaps Zarowski). The fourth name of the boy baptized in 1905 is illegible, and the names of the godparents of 1909 are partly covered by a pasted-on slip of paper.

What we don’t know

  • Where the eldest son, Hubert, was baptized.
  • Who Julia Bojanowska was, the midwife who delivered the first two daughters.
  • How the baptism of the youngest son, born in 1914, was recorded. The copies of the baptism registers end in 1911.
