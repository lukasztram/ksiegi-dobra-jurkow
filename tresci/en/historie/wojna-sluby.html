We know the registers of the neighboring parish of Mszana Dolna from their annual copies. The copies of the baptisms break off in 1911, those of the deaths in 1912. Only the marriages reach 1916. So World War I can be seen in these registers in one place only: in who stood at the altar.

Before the war, not counting Kasinka Mała, nearly fifty couples a year married in the parish. In 1914 there were 41, in 1915 ten, in 1916 nineteen.

The last Carnival

The year 1914 began as usual. In February alone, before Lent, 23 couples married (why precisely then is told in the story {{tytul:wesela}}). There were still four weddings in July. The last took place on July 27, the day before war was declared on Serbia.

For young grooms the priest had for years been adding the same formula: the district office in Limanowa had ruled that “ustawy wojskowe nie stoją na przeszkodzie,” the military laws do not stand in the way of the marriage. Anyone liable for military service could not marry without such a certificate (the story {{tytul:zgoda-pulku}}). In July 1914 a teacher and two young farmers still received one.

From August to December 1914 four weddings were recorded in the whole parish.

“Urgente bello”

The last of them took place on November 14, 1914. A thirty-five-year-old court clerk was marrying a nineteen-year-old young woman from Mszana Dolna. Normally the banns had to be announced three times before a wedding, and a dispensation from them was granted by the bishop’s curia. This time the parish priest, Father Wincenty Jankowski, added a few sentences in Latin beneath the record.

The episcopal see was Tarnów. Four days earlier, on November 10, Russian troops had taken it, and they stayed there until May 1915.

The front then came close to Mszana as well. At the end of November the Austrian cavalry had to leave Dobra and fall back toward Kasina Wielka, a few kilometers from the town. In the first days of December Austrian and German units detrained at the station in Mszana Dolna and set out from there for the Battle of Limanowa. There was a field hospital in Mszana. In the local war cemetery lie 32 soldiers of three armies.

1915: ten weddings

In February 1915, during Carnival, there was not a single wedding in the parish. A year earlier there had been 23. In Mszana Dolna itself nobody married between January 6 and September 11.

More important than the number is who was marrying. Before the war, in 1905–1913, half of the grooms were under 29, and one in five was a widower. Of the ten grooms of 1915, eight were widowers and six were over forty. On May 25 in Słomka the sixty-two-year-old widower Jakub Klimek married. His wife, the widow Agnieszka Michalak, was 59. In November, also in Słomka, a sixty-three-year-old widower married.

The young men were in the army. General mobilization was announced on July 31, 1914, and the Landsturm, the territorial reserve, immediately took in men up to the age of 42. In 1915 eighteen-year-olds were called up, and the older age groups were examined once again.

1916: papers from the army

In 1916 there were nineteen weddings. Eleven of the grooms were widowers, and half were over fifty. The oldest, Jan Tomiczak of Mszana Górna, was seventy. On May 31 he married a forty-six-year-old widow.

The younger men brought certificates of a kind not seen in this register before the war. A twenty-nine-year-old farmer from near Tarnów, who married in Słomka in May, had a letter from the military recruiting district command.

For a twenty-five-year-old born in Kraków who married in October, the priest noted that the district office in Kraków had found him “za niezdolnego do służby wojskowej,” unfit for military service.

In the same month a widow whose husband had died in America married again. Her story is told in {{tytul:peszt-chicago}}.

What came next, we do not know. The copy for 1916 is the last.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe number of weddings in successive years and months. The two notes quoted in the boxes and the 1915 weddings in Słomka: these I checked against the scan.
  • ProbableThe ages and marital status of the grooms of 1915–1916 and the comparison with 1905–1913: counted from the transcriptions, as of November 2026. Individual digits of the ages may have been misread.
  • PossibleThat older widowers married more often than usual during the war. The numbers are small; the only certain thing is that the young men were missing. I estimate the distance of the front from Mszana at the end of November 1914 from a map.
  • To be checkedThe day of the November 1914 wedding (the 14th?). The absence of Kasinka Mała: there are no copies for it for 1910–1916, so all the figures are given without it.

What we don’t know

  • How many men from the parish went to war and how many did not come back. The copies of the death registers end in 1912.
  • What 1917 and 1918 looked like.
  • Whether it was similar in Kasinka Mała.
