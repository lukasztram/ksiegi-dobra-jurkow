In 1882–1884 the Austrian State Railways built the Galician Transversal Railway. From Chabówka to Nowy Sącz the track was laid through Mszana Dolna, Kasina Wielka, Dobra, and Limanowa, and the line opened in December 1884. People from all over the monarchy came to the parish to work on the construction, and the registers recorded their traces.

Builders from afar

In August 1883 a son of Fryderyk Lorenz, from a family of railroad men, was baptized in Dobra; his mother came from Mecklenburg in Prussia. Among the godparents were newcomers from Tyrol, from Italy, and from Spiš.

In September 1884 Karol Ludwik was born, the son of Dionizy Mazzoran, a stonemason from the parish of Tambre in the province of Belluno, Italy, and of Joanna, née Rinaldo. The godparents were Italians too. At that time stonemasons from northern Italy were building bridges and culverts all over the monarchy.

The construction had its price too. In July 1884, 43-year-old Emilia Hampel of Banská Bystrica in Hungary, wife of Wacław, a foreman of the railroad laborers, died after giving birth. Her daughter had been stillborn the day before. That same year the wives and daughters of railroad laborers from Ryczów near Wadowice and from Kamienna in the parish of Trzciana died of pneumonia. In May 1885 three-year-old Karol died of diphtheria; he was the son of Melchior Metzger, an architect on the railroad, and had been born in Halász in Hungary.

Weddings during the construction

On February 2, 1885, in Porąbka, 25-year-old Cesare Gasperi, a foreman of the railroad laborers, from Lavarone in Tyrol, married sixteen-year-old Kunegunda Kalita, daughter of Jan, a full-holding peasant, and Helena, née Podgórna. The witnesses were two architects, Izydor Foramitti and Alojzy Ganz. The bride’s father entered his consent in the register in Polish.

Ten days later, 30-year-old Józef Janota, a supervisor on the railroad construction, from Lomnitz in Bohemia, and Maria Koblížek, also from Bohemia, were married in Dobra. They were close relatives, so they needed a dispensation from the Apostolic Nunciature in Vienna. The witness was the organist Kazimierz Mendalski.

The railroad stays

After the construction, railroad men stayed on along the tracks. In 1890 Józef Kalita, a farmer’s son, was a railroad watchman. In 1893 the stationmaster in Dobra was Izaak Bier, whose son is the subject of the story {{tytul:sasiedzi}}.

Steam engines came along with the railroad. In 1890–1892 Aleksander Maliszewski lived in Półrzeczki, “ductor machinae igneae ad secturam ligni,” the operator of a steam saw for cutting timber. In 1901 the register in Dobra names Wojciech Pyka, who was in charge of a steam engine. Timber from the Beskid forests could now be sawn on the spot and shipped out by rail.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe occupations, origins, and dates of the people mentioned. The transcriptions were checked against the scans.
  • ProbableThe opening date of the line, December 1884. It is given by sources on the railroad.

What we don’t know

  • How many laborers worked on the construction and how many of them were local.
