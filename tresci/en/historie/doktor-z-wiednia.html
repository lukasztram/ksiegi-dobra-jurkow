In the parish, the year 1855 began with destitution. In the hungry months before the new harvest, the priest wrote “ob miseriam,” from destitution, next to one death after another. In summer came cholera. In the four villages whose death registers have already been transcribed—Dobra, Jurków, Chyszówki, and Półrzeczki—93 people were buried in July. In the other months of that year about 20 were buried on average. For 64 of the July deaths the priest recorded cholera. In August there were 19 more such deaths, and 88 in the whole year.

The Austrian authorities sent doctors to places like this. The parish priest entered one of them in the death register of Dobra as number 81.

Doctor Winter died on August 4, 1855, in house no. 99 in Dobra. He was 34. In the cause-of-death column the priest wrote not cholera but typhus. Before he died, the doctor received the sacraments, and the next day the parish priest of Dobra buried him.

That summer typhus and cholera went hand in hand. In Chyszówki, in house no. 47, Teresa and Wojciech Mrozek died of typhus in April, and in July their children died of cholera: five-year-old Magdalena and eighteen-year-old Józef. A doctor who visited the sick was just as exposed as they were.

Maciej Winter was not from here. He had no family in the parish, so no one kept stories about him. All we know is that he came to help and that he stayed in Dobra forever.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe content of the entry about the doctor: name, age, house, cause of death, and burial. The transcription was checked against the scan.
  • To be checkedThe death counts come from a machine transcription of the registers of four villages and have not yet been checked entry by entry.

What we don’t know

  • Where Doctor Winter came from, when he arrived, and how many of the sick he helped. A brief search online turned up nothing about him.
  • Whether he has a grave in Dobra.
  • A trace may survive in Viennese medical journals from 1855 or in the files of the district (Kreis) office in Nowy Sącz.
