Józef Baczyński, known as Skawicki, came from Skawica at the foot of Babia Góra. He was born into a poor family of landless lodgers and in his youth worked as a servant for wealthier farmers. In April 1732 he and his companions robbed a rich tailor. Then he joined the band of the Giertuga brothers, and in 1733 they raided the brewery in Dobczyce together. When the Giertugas were caught, Baczyński became the “harnaś,” that is, the leader of the band. His men raided manors in Jaszczurowa, Śleszowice, Tłuczań, and Łętownia.

According to published accounts, the band had hideouts on the slopes of Mogielica, in Ochotnica, and by the Biała Woda. Mogielica is the mountain at whose foot Półrzeczki and Chyszówki lie, so the brigands knew our area well.

The last party

In late autumn 1735 the brigands came to our area. Accounts of what happened then differ in the details. According to one, they kidnapped a fiddler, brought him to an inn in Dobra, and threw a wild party, which was cut short when the “harnicy” burst in. According to another, they took a musician from the house of a certain Stoczek in Kasinka, came to a cottage in the hamlet of Wilczyska in Dobra, and fell asleep after a lavish feast. In the morning the harnicy appeared without warning.

Harnicy (singular: harnik) was the name for units formed to fight brigands. Several of the companions escaped. Baczyński was captured with one of his comrades, or according to other accounts with four.

According to Wikipedia, the harnicy of Lady Wielopolska, the starost’s wife, delivered the captives to her castle in Sucha. At that time Sucha was linked with the Dobra estate: in 1725 the lady of the manor of Dobra, Anna Małachowska, signed an order to the village head of Dobra “at Sucha Castle.” Other accounts say the brigands were imprisoned in the castle in Kraków. There the castle court sentenced Baczyński to death by beheading, and early in 1736 the sentence was carried out.

What Jurków remembers

Jurków has kept its own memory of this event. According to local residents, the inn where the harnaś was captured stood in Szerszenie, in Jurków Dolny, about 50 meters from today’s chapel of 1826. Some even say the chapel was built on the site of the inn, but others deny it.

The chapel stands opposite house no. 5, the farm of my ancestors, the Podgórni family. They themselves came to Jurków a few years after the harnaś was captured, and house numbers were assigned only in the second half of the 18th century. But Jurków belonged to the same estate as Dobra, so “an inn on the Dobra estate” could have stood here too.

One family from Szerszenie has preserved a story that the harnicy ordered their ancestor to provide a cart and team and drive the brigand to Sucha. They traveled all night. This family memory agrees with the version about the castle in Sucha.

In Jurków it is also said that the inn was kept by a Jew. In the 1730s there were indeed Jewish leaseholders on the Dobra estate. In 1739 the manor steward explained himself over one of the brawls: “I ran to the brewery in all haste to alert the Jews.” Of the innkeepers of those years we know Szymon Wrona by name, and in 1731 witnesses mentioned an innkeeper named Węgrzyn from Wilczyce. None of the sources examined says who kept the inn where Baczyński was captured.

A trace in the records

The only certain trace of this event in the documents of our estate dates from 1739. Among the 41 charges against the steward of the Dobra estate is one that he kept for himself some of the stolen goods recovered “after the capture of the harnaś Baczyński.” This is the subject of the story {{tytul:rzadca}}.

The parish registers are silent about the harnaś. The death registers begin only in 1779, and the baptism register of those years shows neither an innkeeper nor a harnik in Jurków. The cadastral map of 1845 marks no inn in Jurków, but by then it may have been long gone.

How certain is this

  • CertainThat in 1739 the loot recovered after the harnaś was captured was one of the charges against the steward of the Dobra estate.
  • ProbableThe capture on the Dobra estate in late autumn 1735 and the execution in Kraków early in 1736. Published accounts agree on this, and the charge of 1739 links the affair to Dobra.
  • PossibleThat the inn stood in Szerszenie in Jurków, about 50 meters from the chapel, and that a man from Szerszenie drove the brigand to Sucha. These are local accounts. Also that “Wilczyska” is today’s Wilczyce or an old name of a field.
  • To be checkedWhether the chapel stands on the site of the inn. Whether it was an inn or a cottage, and how many brigands were captured.

What we don’t know

  • Where exactly the inn stood and who kept it.
  • What Baczyński told the court about his last days of freedom. His testimony was published by Stanisław Szczotka, in 1936 in the journal “Lud” (The Folk) and in 1952 in “Materiały do dziejów zbójnictwa góralskiego” (Materials for the History of Highland Brigandage). It has not yet been possible to examine these editions. They may give the name of the village and the place.
