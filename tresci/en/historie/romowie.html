The parish registers also recorded Roma. The priest called them “Cingani” in Latin, and later “Cyganie” in Polish, that is, Gypsies. Today we say Roma. In our area these were mainly the Carpathian Roma. From the 15th century on they migrated along the arc of the Carpathians from Wallachia, Hungary, and Slovakia, and over time settled in southern Poland. They were smiths and musicians. To this day they live in Podhale, in the Nowy Sącz region, and around Limanowa, among other places.

On the word of a fellow Rom

The oldest such entry dates from January 13, 1730. The priest baptized Sebastian, son of Łukasz and Anna Wacławowicz, in Dobra. He added that he had accepted them as a lawfully married couple on the word of their companion, also a Rom. The godparents were the nobly born Stanisław Budziński, administrator of the Dobra estate, and his wife Anna.

The administrator Budziński also appears in the story {{tytul:rzadca}}. He ran the estate a few years before the steward Święcicki.

Wanderers in the 19th century

In March 1847 two-year-old Wiktoria, daughter of Antoni Szczepaniak and Anna, died of smallpox in Dobra. The priest recorded the father as “vagabundus,” a wanderer, and added in parentheses: “Cygani,” Gypsies. It was a year of famine and of one of the smallpox epidemics, which are the subject of the stories {{tytul:rok-glodu}} and {{tytul:ospa}}.

On December 2, 1852, in Chyszówki, Rozalia was baptized, daughter of Józef and Anna, “vagabundi seu Cygani,” wanderers, that is, Gypsies. Both came from a place whose name cannot be fully read. The priest recorded that they were married. Their daughter was held at the font by local farmers, Andrzej Zawada and Kunegunda Ranosz, and the midwife at the birth was Marianna Zawada.

In 1864 the daughter of another wandering Roma couple was baptized in Chyszówki. Again the godparents were neighbors from Chyszówki.

Settled in Dobra

By the early 20th century Roma were living in Dobra permanently, in several houses. In 1914 a seventy-year-old Roma woman died here. In 1922, at a baptism, the priest recorded the godparents with a single word: “cygani,” Gypsies. The surname Mirga, one of the typical surnames of the Carpathian Roma, appears in the Dobra registers. Roma families are still in the registers in the early 1940s.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe entries from 1730, 1847, 1852, and 1864. The transcriptions were checked against the scans.
  • ProbableThe background on the Carpathian Roma, based on a study of the Roma minority in Poland.
  • To be checkedWhere Rozalia’s parents of 1852 came from. The place name is written indistinctly.

What we don’t know

  • How many Roma lived in the parish in different years and what they did for a living here. The registers do not give their occupations.
  • What became of the Wacławowicz family of 1730 and of the 19th-century families.
