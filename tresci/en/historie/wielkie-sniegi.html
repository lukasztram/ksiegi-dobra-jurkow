On November 13, 1854, 49-year-old Katarzyna Jarosz of Dobra, wife of Jan, a farmer with a quarter holding, was found in the snow in Tymbark. The priest wrote briefly: “nive suffocata in Tymbark reperta,” smothered by snow, found in Tymbark. A day later the snow killed 26-year-old Gertruda Mrozek in Chyszówki, wife of the landless lodger Piotr. A doctor examined her body.

On December 17, nineteen-year-old Małgorzata Jarosz of Dobra, daughter of the farmer Józef, died in the snow. The priest called her a beggar. These were years of destitution, and many people went begging then. She was buried in Wilkowisko, in the neighboring parish.

Snowdrifts cut the cottages off from the church. In Półrzeczki, in house no. 44, the landless lodger Antoni Olejarz died of dropsy on January 4, 1855. A week later his wife Małgorzata, née Kudlacz, died. For her the priest entered “ob miseriam,” of destitution. For both he added the same thing: they died without the sacraments, because no one could get through to them.

Spring uncovered one more victim. On April 6, 1855, a dead girl of about eighteen was found in a field in Jurków. Nobody knew who she was, so the priest recorded her as “N. N.,” an unknown person. As the cause of death he entered “nive suffocata,” smothered by snow. A doctor examined the body, and on April 10 the parish priest buried her.

This winter came in the middle of the worst years of the 19th century. In 1847 famine and typhus decimated the parish. In 1853–1855 the priest entered death from destitution again and again, and in the summer of 1855 cholera came. A starving person withstands frost poorly, and those who went begging or looking for work in another village did not always come back.

Other winters in the registers

  • February 21, 1838 · DobraEighteen-year-old Alojzy Chochoł, “congelatus inventus,” found frozen.
  • February 15, 1843 · DobraForty-one-year-old Szymon Michalski, “ob nimium frigus mortuus,” died of excessive cold.
  • January 23, 1848 · ChyszówkiThirteen-year-old Jan Drzyzga, “inventus frigore enectus,” found killed by the cold. It was the winter after the year of hunger.
  • December 13, 1855 · ChyszówkiThe 62-year-old widow Regina Czyrnek died without the sacraments “ob nives magnas,” because of the great snows.
  • March 26, 1877 · PółrzeczkiEighteen-year-old Józef Barcz died of typhus without the sacraments “ob magnas nives.”
  • March 13, 1907 · DobraThe 65-year-old widower Jan Judka died of “zamrożenia” (freezing). By then the priest was writing in Polish.

The winters after the year of hunger, when frost killed beggars on the roads, are the subject of the story {{tytul:zamarznieci}}.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe dates, names, ages, and causes of death in all the entries quoted. The transcriptions were checked against the scans.

What we don’t know

  • Whether the winter of 1854/55 was exceptionally snowy throughout Galicia (the Austrian part of partitioned Poland). Newspapers and chronicles of the time may tell.
  • Why Katarzyna Jarosz was going to Tymbark.
  • Who the girl found in the field in Jurków was.
