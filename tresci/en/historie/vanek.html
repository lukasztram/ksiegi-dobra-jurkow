On June 18, 1895, the parish priest Aleksander Bogusz baptized a boy in Dobra who had been born on May 27 in house no. 202. The boy was given three names: Włodzimierz Franciszek Józef. His father, Włodzimierz Vaněk, was the accountant of the Dobra estate. He came from Moravia, where his father Franciszek was an estate controller on the Frain estate, today Vranov nad Dyjí. His mother, Zofia, was the daughter of Franciszek Wesely, the owner of an inn in Dobra.

The godfather was the grandfather from Moravia. In 1894–1904 the Vaněks had at least seven children baptized in Dobra and gave them Czech names: Otakar, Jarosław, Mojmir. The inn and its leaseholders are the subject of the story {{tytul:karczmy}}.

Notes in the margin

Years later, someone added two pieces of news next to this baptism entry, in different ink. One says that on July 4, 1925, in Prague, the boy from house no. 202 married Martha Petit le Brun. The other gives his title, “Envoyé Extraordinaire et Ministre Plénipotentiaire,” that is, envoy extraordinary and minister plenipotentiary, with an address in Prague and the year 1938. Such notes were usually made when a certificate was issued from the register. Later still, a cross and a date appeared next to the names: October 6, 1965, Rome.

The life of Vladimír Vaněk

The rest of his life is described by the archive of the Czech Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The family moved to Bohemia, and in 1913 Vladimír passed his school-leaving examination at the Realschule and commercial school in Karlín near Prague. The war found him in Russia, where he joined the Czech Druzhina, a volunteer unit of Czechs fighting on the Russian side. Drafted into the Austrian army in 1915, he again went over to the Russians. Then he was in London and in Paris, and in 1918 he helped Milan Štefánik form the Czechoslovak legions in Italy. He described his wartime adventures in the book “Moje válečná odysea” (My War Odyssey), published in 1924.

In 1919–1920 he helped build the military intelligence service of the new Czechoslovakia. Then he became a diplomat, serving in Stockholm and in Paris. On March 17, 1939, two days after the Germans occupied the Czech lands, he left again for Sweden, and from there he organized intelligence and communications for the Czechoslovak resistance. In March 1942, at the demand of the German ambassador, the Swedes arrested him and held him until January 1944. From September 1944 to January 1946 he was the Czechoslovak government’s envoy to Italy.

After the Communists took power in 1948 he lost his post at the ministry. In December of that year he emigrated with his family to Italy. He wrote books, among them a biography of Jan Masaryk that was not published until 1994. He died in Rome on October 6, 1965, just as the margin of the register in Dobra records.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe baptism, the parents, the grandparents, and the notes in the margin. The transcriptions were checked against the scan. That the diplomat Vladimír Vaněk is the boy from Dobra is stated by the note in the register itself, and the Czech archive gives his birthplace as Dobra in Galicia.
  • ProbableThe course of his life, according to the biographical entry of the Czech Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Wikipedia.
  • To be checkedThe date of birth: the register gives May 27, his biographers May 28, 1895.

What we don’t know

  • When the Vaněks left Dobra and whether Vladimír ever visited it later.
  • Who asked the parish for his certificate, and when.
