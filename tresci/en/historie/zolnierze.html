After the Partitions of Poland the parish found itself in Galicia, in the Austrian monarchy, and peasants’ sons began to be taken into the emperor’s army. Service lasted a long time. The death registers do not say how many men from our villages went into the army. But they did record those who came home sick, those whom death caught far from home, and soldiers from elsewhere who died while passing through Dobra.

The army reads the registers

On February 18 and 19, 1799, someone in an unfamiliar hand added the same formula and a signature below the entries in the death registers of Jurków, Dobra, and Półrzeczki. In Półrzeczki it was signed by a lieutenant. The army was correcting its conscription lists against the parish registers. It needed to know which of the men were still alive.

They came home sick

In 1833–1895 the registers record more than twenty soldiers who died in the parish or near it. Most were young, unmarried men, and they died of consumption and pneumonia. The priest recorded their regiment, battalion, and company.

  • Dobra · 1833Józef Bolisęga, called Jarosz, 21, a private in the 3rd Archduke Charles Uhlan Regiment, 2nd squadron.
  • Jurków · 1838Jan Smoleń, 32, a soldier of the territorial reserve, the Landwehr, attached to the Imperial-Royal Infantry Regiment No. 20. He died of typhus.
  • Chyszówki · 1839Stanisław Czopek, 25, a private in the Imperial-Royal Infantry Regiment No. 57. He died of consumption.
  • Dobra · 1844Jan Przybytek, 41, of the Landwehr of Regiment No. 20. He was found dead in Tymbark and buried there.

The regiment that recurs most often in the registers is the Imperial-Royal Infantry Regiment No. 20. In 1847 Gustaw Antoni, not quite two years old, died in Dobra; he was the son of Jan Melichar, an army doctor of that regiment in Nowy Sącz.

Some men, after many years of service, came back to the village as patented invalids, that is, with an official document that entitled them to a small allowance. The priest recorded them as “patentalis invalidus.” In 1824–1899 there are a dozen or so of them in the registers. Jan Smoleński of Jurków, an invalid and a widower, died of old age in 1868, at the age of 84.

Far from home

Of others, only an official letter remains. The uhlan Jan Zawada of Chyszówki died of consumption in the military hospital in Vienna. The news reached the parish through the imperial-royal district office (“starostwo”) in Limanowa.

It also happened the other way around. On August 7, 1855, Maciej Kozma, a soldier of the 11th company of the Archduke Charles Ferdinand Infantry Regiment No. 51, died of cholera in Dobra. He had been born in Transylvania. The priest added that he died “in transitu cum cohorte,” while marching through with his unit. This was in the same cholera summer that is the subject of the story {{tytul:doktor-z-wiednia}}. In 1916 the Dobra register recorded the death of a 42-year-old soldier, Jan Mariasz, born in Sieklówka Górna near Jasło.

The Great War

The registers learned of those killed in the First World War from military letters, often years later. The Chyszówki register has three such entries.

  • November 27, 1914Józef Smoleń, born in 1890, a platoon sergeant of the 20th Infantry Regiment, was killed on the Nida River. The notice was sent in May 1919 from Nowy Sącz by the reserve battalion of the 1st Podhale Rifles Regiment.
  • February 6, 1917Stanisław Tomera, son of Pankracy and Katarzyna, née Mrozek, 19, fell in the battle at Capotesti in Romania and was buried there. He was entered in the register only in 1925.
  • July 4, 1918Józef Smoleń, born in 1894, a rifleman of the Imperial-Royal 32nd Rifle Regiment, farmer, bachelor. An army chaplain reported in German that he “an der ital. Front den Heldentod fand,” died a hero’s death on the Italian front.

In 1920, in the war against the Bolsheviks, two men were killed: 22-year-old Jakub Sołtys of Dobra, who was buried by an army chaplain, and Stanisław Michałek of Chyszówki, killed in battle with the Bolshevik army. By then they were soldiers of Poland.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe names, ages, regiments, and causes of death of the soldiers mentioned. Readings compared with the scans.
  • ProbableThe meaning of the 1799 note as a check of the conscription list, and the meaning of the words “patentalis invalidus.”
  • To be checkedThe first name of the soldier of 1855 and his village in Transylvania, and also the place of the 1920 battle, which could not be read.

What we don’t know

  • How many men from the parish served in the army and how many came back. The registers record only the dead.
  • How many of those killed in the First World War never made it into the registers. The Dobra registers for 1914–1915 contain not a single entry about a fallen soldier.
