In the cause-of-death column the priest usually wrote a single word. On July 7, 1826, for thirty-year-old Józef Pałka of house no. 17 in Jurków, he wrote a whole sentence.

One line below, with the same date, is ten-year-old Marianna, daughter of Szymon and Katarzyna Cyrek of house no. 67. For her the priest wrote only: “Eadem morte ut supra,” by the same death as above. One bolt of lightning killed a grown man and a child from two different houses. Whether they were together, in a field or under the same tree, the register does not say.

Eighty years later, on September 6, 1906, lightning killed twenty-year-old Marianna Miśkowiec of house no. 139 in Jurków, daughter of Stefan and Katarzyna, née Stokłosa. By then the priest wrote this entry in Polish: “od piorunu zabita,” killed by lightning. From 1901 the number of the card from the official inspection of the body was recorded for every death, so here too there is the card number and the doctor’s name.

The strangest entry is from Dobra. On February 9, 1920, 29-year-old Jan Suchała of house no. 337 was killed; he left a wife, Marianna, née Przybyłek. The priest wrote: “zabity od Pioruna,” killed by lightning. A thunderstorm in the middle of winter is rare, but the entry is clear.

Lightning accounts for only some of the accidents the registers record. Most often the victims were children who drowned in a stream or a well. Adults were crushed by trees in the forest and by carts, and in winter the frost killed, as told in the story {{tytul:wielkie-sniegi}}.

How certain is this

  • CertainThe dates, names, ages, and causes of death in all four entries. The transcriptions were checked against the scans.

What we don’t know

  • Where the lightning struck Józef Pałka and Marianna Cyrek, and whether they were together.
  • Whether a thunderstorm really passed over Dobra in February 1920. Newspapers from those days could confirm it.
